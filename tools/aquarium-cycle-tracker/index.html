The 4 Stages of the Nitrogen Cycle

Cycling grows the bacteria that turn fish waste into something fish can live with. Ammonia from waste and food is turned into nitrite, and nitrite is turned into nitrate, which you remove with water changes. You can read the stage straight from your test kit:

StageWhat the test showsTypical timing
1. AmmoniaAmmonia present, nitrite 0, nitrate 0Days 1 to about 10
2. Nitrite spikeAmmonia starts falling, nitrite risesUsually weeks 2 to 3
3. Nitrate risingNitrite falling, nitrate climbingWeeks 3 to 6
4. CycledAmmonia 0 and nitrite 0 within 24 hours of a 2 ppm ammonia dose, nitrate presentMost tanks in 4 to 6 weeks; faster with seeded media

Bacteria grow faster in warm water (around 77–86°F), at a pH above about 7, and with oxygen. A pH that falls below 6.5 can stall the cycle. If yours stops moving for a week, test pH and KH first.

Fishless Cycling: What to Do Each Day

Starting from scratch? How to cycle a fish tank has the full method, an ammonia dose calculator and fixes for a stalled cycle. The short version:

  • Dose ammonia to about 2 ppm on day 1, using the amount on your bottle's label for your tank volume. Test the next day.
  • Top it back up to 2 ppm whenever ammonia drops below about 0.5 ppm. More is not faster: above 4 ppm can slow the bacteria.
  • Leave the water alone unless nitrite climbs above about 5 ppm or pH falls. Then a 50% water change helps it restart.
  • Test the finish: dose to 2 ppm and test 24 hours later. Ammonia 0 and nitrite 0 means it is done. Change most of the water to bring nitrate under 20 ppm before you add fish.

Fish-In Cycling: Keep the Fish Safe

With fish already in the tank, the goal is to keep ammonia plus nitrite at or below about 0.5 ppm while the bacteria grow. Test every day, change 25–50% of the water whenever the combined reading goes over that, feed a small amount every other day and add no new fish until you get a week of 0 ammonia and 0 nitrite. Use a dechlorinator that also detoxifies ammonia between changes. The new tank syndrome guide has the day-by-day plan. If a fish shows signs of trouble, use the fish symptom checker.

After the Cycle Finishes

Add fish a few at a time, a week or two apart, so the bacteria can catch up with each new load. Keep testing every 2 to 3 days for the first month. FAQ

How long does it take to cycle a fish tank?

Most tanks take 4 to 6 weeks. Fishless cycling with ammonia often finishes in 3 to 6 weeks, and adding media from an established filter can cut that to 1 to 2 weeks. Cold water, low pH and too much ammonia slow it down.

How do I know when my tank is cycled?

Dose ammonia to about 2 ppm and test 24 hours later. If ammonia and nitrite both read 0 and nitrate is present, the tank is cycled. In a fish-in cycle, a week of daily 0 ammonia and 0 nitrite readings means the same.

Why has my nitrite been high for weeks?

The nitrite stage is often the longest. Very high nitrite, above about 5 ppm, and a falling pH can both stall it. A 50% water change and keeping pH above 7 usually gets it moving again.

Do I need to do water changes while cycling?

In a fishless cycle, only if nitrite gets very high or pH drops. In a fish-in cycle, yes: change 25–50% of the water whenever ammonia plus nitrite goes above about 0.5 ppm.

Does bottled bacteria really work?

It can shorten cycling, but results vary between products and batches. Keep testing as if you had not added it, and trust the readings rather than the label's timeline.
