Take depth readings across the pond and average them. Shelves and sloped sides pull the average well below the deepest point.

How Much Water Does a Koi Pond Hold?

Pond volume is surface area multiplied by average depth. Everything else in koi keeping is downstream of that number: stocking density is quoted in gallons per fish, pump flow is quoted as turnovers of the pond volume per hour, medication and salt doses are quoted per hundred or per thousand gallons, and filter ratings are quoted against pond volume too. Get the volume wrong and every one of those figures is wrong by the same margin.

Two habits cause most of the error. The first is measuring the hole rather than the water — the excavation is bigger than the finished pond once the liner, underlay and any block work are in. The second is using the deepest point as the depth. A pond with 12 inch marginal shelves around a 4 foot centre can average barely 2.5 feet, so treating it as a 4 foot pond overstates the volume by more than half and leaves the filter and pump badly undersized.

If the pond is already full, there is a check that beats any calculation: fit a water meter to the hose, or count the fills of a container of known volume, and record the total as you fill. Use the calculator to plan and the meter reading to confirm.

Koi pond volume formulas

These are the formulas behind the calculator, if you want to work a figure out by hand or check one you have been given.

Rectangular pond
length (ft) × width (ft) × average depth (ft) × 7.48 = US gallons
Circular pond
3.1416 × radius (ft)² × average depth (ft) × 7.48 = US gallons
Oval pond
3.1416 × (length ÷ 2) × (width ÷ 2) × average depth × 7.48 = US gallons
Irregular or kidney shaped pond
length × width × average depth × 0.85 × 7.48 = US gallons

The 7.48 is the number of US gallons in a cubic foot. For imperial gallons use 6.23, and for litres use 28.32. Working in metres instead, a cubic metre is 1,000 litres or about 264 US gallons.

What Size Pond Do Koi Need?

Koi are not garden pond goldfish. A well-kept koi reaches 18 to 24 inches, and jumbo bloodlines pass 30 inches, so the pond has to be planned for the adult fish rather than the 4 inch one you bring home. The widely used minimums among koi keepers are:

  • 1,000 US gallons minimum for a pond that will hold koi at all, and that is a starter figure rather than a comfortable one.
  • 3 feet minimum depth, with 4 feet or more in regions that freeze or that get intense summer sun.
  • 250 US gallons per mature koi as a long-term stocking figure, or around 150 gallons per fish on a pond with a bottom drain, a properly sized filter and dedicated aeration.
  • Length over depth for swimming: koi swim horizontally, so a long, wide pond beats a deep, narrow one of the same volume.

Depth does more than add gallons. It gives fish a thermally stable layer to sit in through both winter and a heatwave, it keeps them out of easy reach of herons and raccoons, and it slows the day-to-night temperature swing that stresses koi in shallow water. A 2 foot pond in an open garden can move several degrees between afternoon and dawn; a 4 foot pond barely moves.

How deep should a koi pond be?

Three feet is the practical floor. Four to five feet is the range most established koi keepers build to, and ponds deeper than about six feet mainly add cost and make netting a sick fish difficult without adding much benefit. In climates with hard winters the requirement is that a substantial body of water stays above freezing beneath the ice, which is a function of both depth and volume — a deep but small pond can still freeze through.

Surface area still matters

Oxygen enters a pond mainly at the surface, so two ponds with the same volume are not equivalent if one is deep and narrow. A wide surface, a waterfall or an air pump all raise dissolved oxygen, and that becomes the limiting factor on warm summer nights when water holds less oxygen and the fish need more of it. The calculator reports surface area alongside volume for this reason.

What Your Pond Volume Tells You Next

Once you have a gallon figure, four decisions follow directly from it.

Stocking

Divide by 250 for a conservative adult stocking level, or by 150 if the pond has strong filtration and aeration. The koi pond stocking calculator adjusts that for the size of your fish and the filtration you actually have.

Pump and filter

A koi pond should turn its full volume over roughly once per hour, so a 3,000 gallon pond wants a pump moving about 3,000 GPH at the head height it is actually working against — not 3,000 GPH on the box. The koi pond pump size calculator works out that head loss for you.

Liner

Liner size comes from the same measurements plus depth and overlap. The koi pond liner calculator gives the sheet size and the nearest standard liner.

Treatments

Salt and medication doses are quoted per volume, and a 20 percent error in your gallon figure is a 20 percent error in every dose. The koi pond salt calculator converts a target concentration into pounds or kilograms of salt.

Common Koi Pond Sizing Mistakes

  • Using the deepest point as the depth. Shelves, slopes and a planted margin all pull the average down. Measure in several places and average.
  • Measuring the excavation. Underlay, liner and block work eat into the hole. Measure the water surface once it is filled, or subtract the build thickness when planning.
  • Forgetting the filter and pipework. A gravity-fed filter chamber, a settlement chamber and the plumbing can hold hundreds of extra gallons. That helps stocking, but it also means salt and medication doses must be calculated on the total system volume, not just the pond.
  • Buying koi for the pond you have today. Six 5-inch koi look lost in a 1,500 gallon pond. The same six fish at 20 inches will be crowded in it.
  • Treating a bigger pump as a substitute for volume. Turnover moves water through the filter; it does not create swimming space or thermal stability.
  • Ignoring imperial versus US gallons. A UK gallon is about 20 percent larger. Product ratings and dosing instructions do not always say which one they mean, so check before dosing.

Methodology and accuracy

Pond volume is calculated as surface area × average depth. Surface area uses length × width for rectangular ponds, π × radius² for circular ponds, π × (length ÷ 2) × (width ÷ 2) for ovals, and length × width × 0.85 for irregular and kidney shapes. The 0.85 factor is a common practical allowance for curved edges, not a measurement of your particular pond — a strongly lobed shape holds less and a nearly rectangular one holds more.

Unit conversions are exact and carried at full precision internally, rounded only for display: 1 ft = 0.3048 m, 1 ft³ = 7.480519 US gallons = 6.228835 imperial gallons = 28.316847 litres, 1 m³ = 1,000 litres = 264.172 US gallons.

The koi capacity, pump flow and liner figures shown alongside the volume are rules of thumb widely used in pond keeping — 250 US gallons per mature koi (150 with strong filtration and aeration), one full turnover per hour for pump flow, and pond dimension plus twice depth plus overlap for liner size. They are planning starting points, not measured results, and the dedicated calculators linked from this page refine each one with the variables that actually change it.

Pump flow shown here is the target flow only. It does not account for head loss, which can reduce a pump’s real output by a third or more; use the pump calculator for a figure you can buy against.
