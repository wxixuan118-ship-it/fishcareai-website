What Common Fish Symptoms Usually Mean

Most aquarium fish illnesses start with the water. Before you buy medicine, test ammonia and nitrite: they rule out the cause that kills the most fish, and they change what every treatment needs to look like.

When It Is an Emergency

Act straight away, and treat the water first, if any of these are true:

  • Ammonia reads 0.25 ppm or more, or nitrite reads above 0.
  • A fish is gasping at the surface, especially with odd swimming or lying on its side.
  • A fish has died in the last few days, or several fish show signs at once.
  • White spots are spreading. Ich can kill a whole tank if it is left for a week.

A water change of 30–50 percent with dechlorinated water at the same temperature is safe for almost every one of these, and it buys time while you work out the cause. If you need a dated plan for a medicine course, use the fish treatment planner.

How the Symptom Checker Works

The risk level follows fixed rules, not a guess. Toxic water (ammonia at or above 0.25 ppm or any nitrite), a recent death, or gasping combined with abnormal swimming is urgent. Gasping, lying on its side, white spots or several sick fish is high. A single mild sign with safe water readings and a fish that still eats is low. Everything else is moderate. Signs that have lasted longer than a week move the level up, because the fish has not recovered on its own.

The likely causes, first steps and warning signs come from the combination you pick, your water readings, whether fish were added recently and how old the tank is. New tanks under six weeks old often have ammonia or nitrite spikes; if yours is one of them, follow the aquarium cycle tracker. FAQ

How do I know what is wrong with my fish?

Write down what you see, how long it has been going on and what changed recently, then test ammonia, nitrite and nitrate. The combination narrows it down: white spots after new fish point to ich, gasping with ammonia in the water points to water poisoning, ragged fins after a crowded month point to fin rot.

Should I do a water change when my fish is sick?

In most cases, yes. A 30–50% change with dechlorinated water at the same temperature lowers ammonia, nitrite and nitrate without stressing the fish. If you are about to dose medicine, change the water first and then dose.

Why is my fish lying at the bottom or on its side?

Common causes are poor water, low oxygen, swim bladder trouble from constipation or overfeeding, and late-stage illness. Test ammonia and nitrite first, check the temperature, and skip feeding for a day if the belly looks swollen.

How long can a fish go without eating?

Most healthy adult aquarium fish can go several days to a week without food, so one skipped meal is not an emergency. A fish that refuses food for more than 2 or 3 days while tank mates eat usually has a water, temperature or health problem.
