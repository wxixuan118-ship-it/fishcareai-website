How to Measure for a Koi Pond Liner

The liner has to run down one side of the pond, across the bottom, up the other side, and then far enough past the edge to be anchored. So the depth is counted twice — once for each side — and the overlap is counted twice as well.

Liner length
pond length + (2 × maximum depth) + (2 × overlap per side)
Liner width
pond width + (2 × maximum depth) + (2 × overlap per side)

A 12 × 8 ft pond that is 4 ft deep with a 1 ft overlap needs 12 + 8 + 2 = 22 ft of length and 8 + 8 + 2 = 18 ft of width, so a 22 × 18 ft sheet. Since liners come in fixed sizes, that becomes a 25 × 20 ft sheet off the shelf.

Three things catch people out:

  • Depth is doubled. Adding the depth once is the most common liner sizing mistake, and it leaves a sheet that reaches the far edge with nothing to anchor.
  • Use maximum depth, not average. Average depth is the right figure for volume; the liner has to reach the bottom of the deepest point.
  • Shelves and slopes need slack. A pond with planting shelves, a stepped profile or steep sides needs the liner to follow more surface than a straight-sided box, so add half a foot on each dimension for a heavily stepped design.

Measure the excavation rather than the intended water surface, and take the measurements after the hole is dug rather than from the plan — few ponds come out exactly as drawn.

The one non-negotiable

Buy liner sold specifically as fish safe or pond grade. Roofing EPDM looks identical and is often cheaper, but many roofing grades contain algaecides, mildewcides or plasticisers that leach into the water and are toxic to fish. The saving is not worth the risk, and the damage is not always obvious until the pond is stocked.

Do I need underlay?

Yes, on any pond you intend to keep. Underlay is a non-woven geotextile that sits between the ground and the liner and protects it from stones, roots and settlement. A liner puncture in a filled, stocked koi pond is a serious problem — draining, finding the hole and patching it means moving the fish — and underlay costs a small fraction of the liner. Buy the same sheet size as the liner, and on stony or clay ground consider a double layer or a sand blinding underneath as well.

Handling the sheet

Liner weight scales quickly. A 25 × 20 ft EPDM sheet is around 150 lb, and a 40 × 30 is nearly 360 lb as delivered — beyond what two people can carry and position, particularly through a garden gate. Check the weight before ordering, plan the access route, and unfold the liner in place rather than carrying it opened. Laying it on a warm day helps: EPDM is much more compliant in the sun than at 5 °C.

Methodology and accuracy

Liner size is calculated as pond dimension + (2 × maximum depth) + (2 × overlap per side), applied to both length and width. This is the standard pond liner formula and assumes the liner runs down one side, across the base and up the other side with the stated overlap anchoring each edge.

The sheet area is the product of the two calculated dimensions and is exact for the figures you enter. It does not add slack for planting shelves, stepped profiles or steeply sloping sides, which need the liner to follow more surface than a straight-sided pond — add roughly half a foot to each dimension for a heavily stepped design.

The nearest standard size is chosen from a list of sizes commonly stocked by liner suppliers, allowing the sheet to be rotated. Ranges vary by supplier and by country, so treat it as a guide to what to look for rather than a catalogue entry, and confirm availability before finalising a pond design around a particular size.

Weight estimates use typical values of 0.30 lb/ft² for 45 mil EPDM, 0.40 for 60 mil EPDM, 0.19 for 30 mil PVC, 0.13 for 20 mil PVC and 0.16 for 45 mil RPE. Actual weights vary between manufacturers, and the delivered roll includes packaging.

Unit conversions are exact: 1 ft = 0.3048 m, 1 ft² = 0.09290304 m², 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg. Material service lives quoted on this page are typical industry figures, not warranties.
