Goldfish Tank Mates by Tank Size

Goldfish tank mates have to handle cooler water than most aquarium fish, and survive a tank mate that eats anything that fits in its mouth. Those two facts rule out most community fish. The table lists the smallest tank where each pairing reliably works.

Tank mateMin. tankWhy it works or fails
20–30 gallons: fancy goldfish and a cleanup crew
Another fancy goldfish30 gal for 2✓ Works:Same cool temperatures, same slow pace, same food. Add about 10 gal for each extra fancy.
Nerite snail20 gal✓ Works:Too hard-shelled to swallow and grazes algae goldfish ignore. Slows down below 65°F.
Mystery snail20 gal⚠ Caution:Survives, but goldfish pull at its antennae and it goes sluggish below 68°F.
White cloud mountain minnow30 gal⚠ Caution:A true coldwater fish that is fast enough for slow fancies, but adult goldfish over 5 in will swallow it.
Zebra danio30 gal⚠ Caution:Handles 65–75°F and is too quick to catch, but it beats slow fancies to every meal.
Hillstream loach30 gal⚠ Caution:Wants the same cool, oxygen-rich water, but also strong flow that fancy goldfish struggle to swim in.
40–55 gallons: faster or larger companions
Weather (dojo) loach55 gal✓ Works:Coldwater, peaceful and too big to eat. It reaches 8–10 in, so it needs the longer tank.
Rosy barb40 gal⚠ Caution:Tolerates 64°F and up and dodges easily, but may nip the long fins of fancy varieties. Better with single-tails.
Bristlenose pleco40 gal⚠ Caution:Prefers 72°F or warmer, and plecos sometimes rasp the slime coat off slow goldfish at night.
Common or comet goldfish75 gal⚠ Caution:Single-tails reach 12 in and outswim fancies to food. Keep single-tails with single-tails.
Ponds only
KoiPond, 1,000+ gal⚠ Caution:Same water and diet, and they live together in ponds. Koi pass 24 in, so never in an aquarium.
Any tank size: never combine
Betta—✗ Avoid:Bettas need 78–80°F and goldfish 65–72°F. Goldfish may also nip the betta's long fins.
Neon tetra—✗ Avoid:A tropical fish, and small enough to be eaten once the goldfish grows.
Guppy—✗ Avoid:A tropical fish that gets chased, nipped and eventually eaten.
Cherry shrimp—✗ Avoid:Goldfish forage constantly and will eat every shrimp they find.
Angelfish—✗ Avoid:Needs 76–82°F and will pick at slow, fancy goldfish.
Oscar and other large cichlids—✗ Avoid:Predatory and tropical. Goldfish are sold as feeder fish for them for a reason.

Risk note: Goldfish keep growing for years, so a fish that is safe with a 2-inch goldfish can be swallowed by a 6-inch one. Re-check every pairing as your goldfish grow, and size the filter for goldfish waste before adding anything.

Where this table and a score in the ranked list below disagree, go by the table. The scores come from water ranges and adult size, and they miss behaviour such as predation and fin nipping.

Choosing Goldfish Tank Mates

Good Goldfish tank mates share the same temperature and pH window, will not be eaten by (or eat) the Goldfish, and do not compete for the same swimming zone or feeding time. Each pair guide below scores those factors from 0 to 100 and gives the minimum tank size for keeping both species together.

Start with the highest-scoring pairs; anything under 45 is listed so you know what to avoid, not as a recommendation.

Care baseline

Classic pond and aquarium fish that prefer cool water. Heavy waste producers that require robust filtration. Will eat small fish and shrimp.
