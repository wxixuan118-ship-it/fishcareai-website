Can Betta Fish Live With Ghost Shrimp?

Use caution

Ghost shrimp may coexist with some bettas, but they are not a dependable cleanup crew for a betta tank. Individual aggression, shrimp size, and the amount of cover determine the outcome.

Why the risk is high

Ghost shrimp are peaceful and inexpensive, but they can be smaller and more exposed than Amano shrimp. A betta may stalk them, nip their antennae, or eat them. “Ghost shrimp” is also a common store label, so identify the shrimp before relying on its adult size.

Recommended setup

  • Use at least a 10-gallon planted aquarium for one betta and a small shrimp group; more space provides a safer buffer.
  • Add dense moss, wood, rocks, and crevices before introduction so shrimp can retreat and molt safely.
  • Feed consistently and observe at introduction and after lights-out. Keep a cycled backup tank ready.
  • Separate immediately for repeated chasing, injured antennae, missing limbs, or constant hiding.

FAQ

Can betta fish live with ghost shrimp?

Sometimes, but this is a high-risk pairing. Ghost shrimp may be harassed or eaten by a betta, and some shrimp sold under this name are actually smaller, more vulnerable species.

What tank size is best?

Use a planted aquarium of at least 10 gallons for one betta and a small shrimp group, with dense cover and a separate backup tank.

What should I do if the betta chases the shrimp?

Separate the fish and shrimp immediately. Repeated chasing, injured antennae, missing limbs, or constant hiding means the setup is not safe.
