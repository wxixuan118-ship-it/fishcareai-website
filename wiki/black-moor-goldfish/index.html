Black Moor Goldfish

Carassius auratus (Black Moor variety) — Linnaeus, 1758 (captive variety)
🏠 Origin: China (selective breeding; wild ancestor from East Asia)
📏 Size: 6–8 in (15–20 cm)
⏳ Lifespan: 10–15+ years
⭐ Care level: Intermediate

The Black Moor goldfish is one of the most striking fancy goldfish varieties — a slow-swimming, velvet-black fish with dramatic protruding telescope eyes that give it an endearingly wide-eyed expression. Despite their delicate appearance, Black Moors are among the more robust fancy goldfish varieties when kept in properly sized, cold, well-filtered tanks. They are not tropical fish and must not be kept with most community aquarium species.

Taxonomy & Classification

The Black Moor is not a separate species but a selectively bred variety of the common goldfish, Carassius auratus — the same species as every other goldfish variety from comets to orandas. It is distinguished by two features: a velvety, jet-black coloration (caused by melanophore cells) and protruding, telescope-style eyes (a genetic mutation affecting eye socket development). The variety originated in China and has been maintained through centuries of selective breeding.

Natural Habitat

As a fully domesticated captive variety, Black Moors have no wild habitat. Their ancestor, the wild carp Carassius auratus, is native to slow-moving, often murky freshwater across East Asia — rivers, lakes, and ponds. Black Moors retain the cold-water preference of their ancestor and are NOT tropical fish.

  • Cold to temperate water: prefer 65–72°F (18–22°C); do not require a heater in most indoor environments
  • Slow-moving water: fancy goldfish varieties are poor swimmers compared to single-tailed goldfish; strong currents stress them
  • High oxygen requirement: goldfish have high metabolisms and need well-oxygenated water
  • No tropical environment: temperatures above 75°F stress fancy goldfish long-term

Tank Setup

A 20-gallon tank is the minimum for a single Black Moor, with 10 additional gallons per additional fish. Fancy goldfish produce heavy waste and require substantial filtration:

  • Oversized filtration: a filter rated for at least twice the tank volume; goldfish produce 2–3× the waste of most tropical fish
  • Smooth decor: avoid sharp decorations — the telescope eyes of Black Moors are fragile and easily scratched; any wound on the eye becomes infected quickly
  • Wide open swimming space: fancy goldfish are clumsy swimmers; they need unobstructed space to maneuver
  • No heater needed: Black Moors prefer cooler water than room temperature in most climates
  • High oxygen: air stone or surface agitation; goldfish need highly oxygenated water

Telescope Eye Care

The Black Moor's most distinctive feature — the protruding telescope eyes — is also its most vulnerable. The eyes protrude far from the socket, making them susceptible to:

  • Physical injury: catching on sharp decorations, rocks, or filter intakes
  • Infection: any wound on the eye quickly develops into Pop-Eye (exophthalmia) or bacterial infection
  • Cloudy eyes: bacterial or water-quality related opacity of the cornea
Eye safety: Audit your tank for anything sharp — smooth rocks only, sand or fine gravel substrate, no sharp plastic plants. Cover filter intakes with foam sponge to prevent the fish's eye from being sucked against the intake.

Black Moors also have significantly reduced vision compared to normal-eyed goldfish, which affects their ability to compete for food at feeding time.

Diet & Feeding

Black Moors are omnivores and enthusiastic eaters. As with all goldfish, overfeeding is a common problem:

  • Sinking pellets preferred: goldfish that gulp air at the surface while eating floating food often develop swim bladder problems; sinking pellets reduce this risk
  • High-quality goldfish formula: wheat germ-based pellets in cool water (under 65°F); higher-protein formulas at warmer temperatures
  • Vegetables: blanched peas (shelled), zucchini, spinach — excellent fiber source and helps prevent constipation
  • Frozen food: blood worms, brine shrimp — in moderation as treats
Tip: Feed only what the fish can consume in 3 minutes, twice daily. Fast for one day per week to prevent constipation — a common problem in fancy goldfish.

Compatible Tank Mates

Black Moors should only be kept with other fancy goldfish varieties of similar swimming ability:

  • Other fancy goldfish (ryukin, oranda, ranchu, telescope eye varieties)
  • Bubble-eye and celestial goldfish (similarly impaired swimmers)
  • Dojo loach / weather loach (tolerates cold water; peaceful scavenger)

Avoid: Single-tailed goldfish (comet, shubunkin, common) — they outcompete Black Moors for food and may nip fins. Tropical fish — temperature incompatibility. Any fast or nippy species.

Common Health Issues

  • Swim bladder disease — floating or sinking abnormally; caused by overfeeding, constipation, or bacterial infection; fast for 2–3 days and feed blanched peas
  • Pop-Eye (Exophthalmia) — eye(s) swelling outward further than normal; bacterial infection; treat with antibacterial medication
  • Eye injury / cloudy eye — physical trauma or bacterial infection; remove sharp objects; treat with antibacterial medication and salt bath
  • Ich — white spots; treat with temperature increase and medication; goldfish tolerate most ich treatments
  • Fin rot — usually secondary to water quality; increase water change frequency and treat with antibacterial medication
Note: Black Moors' black coloration may shift to orange-gold as they age, particularly in warmer water or under intense light — this is a normal, non-pathological color change and does not indicate illness.
