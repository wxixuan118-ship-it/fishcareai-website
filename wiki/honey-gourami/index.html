Honey Gourami

Trichogaster chuna — Hamilton, 1822
🏠 Origin: India and Bangladesh (Brahmaputra, Ganges basins)
📏 Size: 1.5–2 in (4–5 cm)
⏳ Lifespan: 4–8 years
⭐ Care level: Beginner

The honey gourami is one of the best-kept secrets in the freshwater hobby. Smaller, shyer, and more disease-resistant than its popular cousin the dwarf gourami, it rewards patient keepers with spectacular honey-orange coloration in breeding males, gentle community-tank behavior, and an endearing personality. At just 1.5–2 inches long, it is perfectly scaled for a planted nano or community tank of 10 gallons or more.

Taxonomy & Classification

The honey gourami belongs to the family Osphronemidae — the gouramis and related labyrinth fishes. It is a close relative of the dwarf gourami (Trichogaster lalius) but remains significantly smaller, considerably less aggressive, and more tolerant of imperfect water conditions, making it one of the best choices for a beginner's planted nano tank.

Natural Habitat

Honey gouramis are native to densely vegetated, slow-moving waters of India and Bangladesh — including weedy ponds, rice paddies, and sluggish streams in the Brahmaputra and Ganges river basins. Like all labyrinth fish, they breathe atmospheric air via a specialized labyrinth organ above their gills.

  • Dense surface vegetation: floating plants like water lettuce and frogbit provide cover for bubble-nest building
  • Shallow, slow water: typically under 3 feet deep, with minimal current
  • Soft to moderately hard water: slightly acidic to neutral pH
  • Seasonal variation: habitats flood dramatically during monsoon; fish tolerate wide temperature and pH swings

Tank Setup

A 10-gallon tank is sufficient for a pair of honey gouramis; a 20-gallon allows a small group (1 male to 2–3 females). Key setup elements:

  • Floating plants: essential for the male to build a bubble nest during breeding and for fish to shelter near the surface
  • Dense mid-level planting: Java fern, Anubias, stem plants — honey gouramis are shy and need plant cover to feel secure
  • Gentle flow: a sponge filter or spray bar diffuser; strong currents stress labyrinth fish
  • Warm temperature: keep temperature stable; avoid cold drafts near the water surface — labyrinth fish breathe surface air and cold surface air can damage the organ
  • Low light: subdued or planted-tank lighting reduces stress

Labyrinth Organ & Breathing

Like all gouramis, honey gouramis possess a labyrinth organ — a complex, lung-like chamber above the gill cavity that allows the fish to extract oxygen directly from atmospheric air. This adaptation evolved in oxygen-poor, stagnant waters.

In the aquarium, honey gouramis will regularly swim to the surface to gulp air — this is entirely normal. The labyrinth organ also enables them to survive briefly out of water, which is why they occasionally jump.

Care note: Always keep the aquarium warm near the surface. Honey gouramis breathe surface air, and consistently cold air above the water (in unheated rooms in winter) can cause respiratory infections in the labyrinth organ.

Diet & Feeding

Honey gouramis are omnivores with a preference for small, protein-rich foods. In the wild they eat zooplankton, small insects, and algae. In captivity:

  • Staple: small pellets or micro-flake formulated for tropical fish
  • Frozen food: blood worms, brine shrimp, daphnia, cyclops — excellent condition food and essential for breeding
  • Live food: micro worms, baby brine shrimp, fruit flies (flightless)
  • Vegetables: blanched spinach or zucchini occasionally; some individuals accept spirulina-based food
Tip: Feed small amounts twice daily. Honey gouramis are shy feeders — ensure food sinks or floats near them; they may lose out to faster tank mates at feeding time.

Compatible Tank Mates

Honey gouramis are among the most peaceful community fish available. Excellent companions include:

  • Nano tetras (ember tetra, chili rasbora, neon tetra)
  • Corydoras catfish
  • Otocinclus
  • Cherry shrimp and neocaridina shrimp (adults generally safe)
  • Nerite snails and mystery snails
  • Other peaceful nano fish

Avoid: Aggressive or nippy fish (tiger barbs, cichlids), larger gouramis (may bully), betta fish (incompatible temperaments).

Common Health Issues

  • Velvet — golden dust-like coating; treat with copper-based medication and reduce light
  • Ich — white spots; temperature increase to 82°F and appropriate medication
  • Bacterial infections — usually linked to poor water quality or stress; maintain clean water and consider antibacterial treatment
  • Labyrinth organ infection — caused by breathing cold surface air; maintain warm, humid space above the water line
Note: Unlike dwarf gouramis, honey gouramis are NOT affected by Dwarf Gourami Iridovirus (DGIV). This makes them a significantly more robust choice for the community aquarium.
