German Blue Ram Care Guide 2026

Mikrogeophagus ramirezi — Myers & Harry, 1948
🏠 Origin: Venezuela & Colombia (Orinoco Basin)
📏 Size: 2–3 in (5–7 cm)
⏳ Lifespan: 2–4 years
⭐ Care level: Intermediate

The German Blue Ram is one of the most spectacularly colored freshwater fish in the world — a jewel-box cichlid that packs extraordinary beauty into a 2–3 inch body. Males display a kaleidoscope of electric blue spots on golden-yellow flanks, a vivid red patch on the abdomen, a black vertical bar through the eye, and a characteristic black-spotted dorsal fin. They are ideal for aquascaped community tanks — peaceful, personable, and genuinely stunning centerpiece fish.

Important: German Blue Rams are sensitive fish that require warm, soft, acidic water and pristine water quality. They are not suitable for new tanks or beginners who have not yet mastered consistent water maintenance. However, for an intermediate keeper with an established planted tank, they are deeply rewarding.

Natural Habitat

Wild rams inhabit the llanos (tropical savannas) of Venezuela and Colombia — shallow, warm, slow-moving waterways with sandy substrates, scattered aquatic plants, and minimal current. Water is very warm (78–85°F), soft (below 5 dGH), and slightly acidic (pH 5.5–6.5). These conditions must be replicated closely for rams to thrive.

Tank Setup

A 20-gallon long tank is the practical minimum for a pair. Planted aquascapes suit rams perfectly: fine sand substrate, scattered flat stones and driftwood (rams spawn on flat surfaces), broad-leaved plants for territory definition, and areas of open sandy bottom for foraging. Subdued lighting enhances their coloration. Gentle filtration — rams dislike current. Note: their high temperature requirement (78–86°F) restricts compatible tank mates.

Diet

Carnivore-leaning omnivores. Feed high-quality dwarf cichlid micro-pellets twice daily; supplement with frozen bloodworms, daphnia, brine shrimp, and cyclops 3–4 times weekly. Live blackworms are excellent conditioning food for breeding. Avoid overfeeding — uneaten food degrades water quality rapidly at their preferred high temperatures.

Breeding

Rams are open substrate spawners that form devoted monogamous pairs. They clean a flat stone, large leaf, or depression in the sand and deposit 100–300 eggs. Both parents share guarding and fanning duties. Eggs hatch in 48–60 hours; larvae become free-swimming in 4–5 days. Both parents herd the fry together and defend them aggressively from tank mates. Feed fry baby brine shrimp nauplii from day 1 of free-swimming. First-time pairs frequently eat eggs — this typically resolves after several spawning attempts.

Common Health Issues

  • Hexamita / HITH — pitting on head and lateral line; treat with metronidazole; improve water quality
  • Internal parasites — common in newly acquired fish; treat with metronidazole + praziquantel in food
  • Ich — white spots; raise temperature to 88°F for 5–7 days (rams tolerate this well)
  • Bacterial infections — secondary to stress or water quality failure
