Flowerhorn Cichlid Care Guide 2026

Flowerhorn hybrid (Cichlidae)
🏠 Origin: Selectively bred; origin Southeast Asia
📏 Size: 12–16 in (30–40 cm)
⏳ Lifespan: 10–12 years
⭐ Care level: Intermediate

The Flowerhorn is one of the most distinctive and controversial fish in the freshwater hobby. Developed in Southeast Asia in the 1990s through selective hybridization of several Central American cichlid species, they are prized for their vivid colors, the dramatic nuchal hump (kok) on the male's forehead, and their remarkable intelligence and owner-recognition behavior. They cannot exist in the wild — no natural population exists.

Solo fish: Flowerhorns are extremely aggressive toward other fish and almost always must be kept alone. Their entire tank is their territory, and they will attack anything introduced — including other Flowerhorns unless carefully managed for breeding.

Tank Requirements

A minimum 75-gallon tank for a single adult Flowerhorn. They grow large (12–16 inches), produce significant waste, and need substantial swimming space. Powerful filtration rated for 3–5x tank volume per hour is essential. Décor should be minimal and secured — Flowerhorns rearrange everything they can move. Use smooth rounded rocks and driftwood; avoid sharp objects that can damage their fins or kok.

Diet

Carnivore-leaning omnivores with hearty appetites. Feed high-quality large cichlid pellets twice daily; supplement with frozen earthworms, shrimp, and krill. Color-enhancing foods (astaxanthin, spirulina) help maintain vivid coloration. "Kok" development (the nuchal hump size) is partly genetic and partly influenced by nutrition and tank conditions.

Common Health Issues

  • HITH — head and lateral line erosion; treat with metronidazole and improve diet/water quality
  • Ich — white spots; raise temperature and treat with medication
  • Bacterial infections — ulcers and fin rot secondary to poor water quality
