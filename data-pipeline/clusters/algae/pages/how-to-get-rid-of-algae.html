How to get rid of algae in fish tank glass, gravel and plants: cut the light to 6–8 hours, get nitrate under 20 ppm, change 25–30% of the water weekly, scrub and siphon, fix the filter flow, add plants and algae eaters, and only then consider algae control drops.

Done together, and in that order, the seven steps clear green film and diatoms in 10 to 14 days and black beard or hair algae in 3 to 4 weeks, and because they remove the cause rather than the symptom the algae stays gone.

Skip a step and the algae comes back, which is why most people are on their third bottle of algaecide and their second pleco. Identify the algae with the algae identification chart, then work through the steps below. The algae eater fish guide covers which animal to add in step 6.

Why Algae Grows in a Fish Tank

Algae needs three things: light, nutrients (nitrate and phosphate from fish waste and food) and CO2. It grows out of control when one is in excess or, in planted tanks, when the ratio swings, because algae adapts to a change in hours and plants take weeks. In practice five causes account for almost every tank algae control problem:

  • Too much light. Lights on 10 to 14 hours, a tank near a window, or a bright LED over a tank with no plants to use it. The single most common cause.
  • Too much food. Uneaten flake becomes phosphate within a day. Overfeeding shows up as green glass before it shows up as sick fish.
  • Nitrate over 20 ppm. Overstocking, skipped water changes, a gravel bed full of mulm. Test with a liquid kit; strips read low.
  • Low or uneven flow. Dead corners and a clogged filter starve plants of CO2 and nutrients while algae, which needs less, moves in. Black beard algae grows where flow drops.
  • An immature tank. Brown diatoms in the first three months are normal and leave on their own once silicates are used up.

Check the tank's numbers against the aquarium water parameters guide before you start; nitrate, phosphate and the hours the light is on tell you which steps matter most.

Aquarium Algae Control Drops

Aquarium algae control drops are one of three chemistries. Copper-based algaecides kill algae and every snail and shrimp in the tank, and stay in the gravel for months. Liquid carbon (glutaraldehyde) products kill black beard and hair algae on contact at 2 to 3× the plant dose but melt vallisneria and some mosses and stress fish at that dose. Barley straw extract and enzyme drops are safe and do very little. None of them lowers light or nitrate, so the algae returns in 3 to 6 weeks unless steps 1 to 5 are done first.

If you use them: half the label dose, an airstone running for 48 hours because dying algae strips oxygen, and a 30% water change three days later. Never dose a tank with shrimp, nerite or mystery snails, or scaleless fish (loaches, some catfish) with a copper product. For a single patch of black beard algae, 3% hydrogen peroxide from a syringe onto the spot with the filter paused is cheaper, safer and works in days; the black beard algae guide gives the dose.

Keeping Algae Out of the Fish Tank

Once the tank is clear, the routine that keeps it clear is short. It is the same routine the fish tank water guide recommends for water quality, because algae is a water quality symptom:

  • Lights on a timer, 7 to 8 hours, and no direct sun.
  • Feed once a day, what disappears in 30 seconds; one fasting day a week.
  • 25% water change every week with a gravel vacuum; nitrate under 20 ppm on a liquid test.
  • Rinse filter sponges monthly in the water you removed; replace a canister filter's floss, never its ceramic media.
  • Wipe the glass weekly with a magnet cleaner before it needs scraping.
  • Keep the grazers fed: an algae wafer twice a week keeps otocinclus and siamese algae eaters alive after they have done their job.
  • In planted tanks, keep CO2 and fertilizer steady; a change in either is an invitation to black beard algae.

A tank that grows a light green film on the glass in a week is healthy; that is the sign the light and nutrients are balanced and the nerites have work. A tank that grows nothing at all is usually under-lit for its plants. The question of how to get rid of algae in fish tank after fish tank stops coming up once the routine is weekly instead of monthly. The RSPCA's fish welfare advice makes the same point from the fish's side: stable, clean water is the whole job.
