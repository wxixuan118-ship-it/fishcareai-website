There are over 100 rainbowfish species, but the types of rainbow fish you will actually find for sale come down to about a dozen, sorted into three size bands: the dwarf species under 2.5 inches (dwarf neon, threadfin, forktail, Gertrude's blue-eye), the medium 3–4.5 inch Melanotaenia that make up most of the hobby (boesemani, turquoise, neon-blue, celebes), and the large 4–6 inch species (Australian, splendid, red, parkinson's). The band tells you the tank; the species tells you the colour; and the care is the same recipe for all of them — a school of six or more, a long tank and hard water.

The Three Groups of Rainbow Fish

  • Dwarf rainbowfish and blue-eyes (Melanotaenia praecox, Iriatherina, Pseudomugil) — 1.5–2.5 inches, schools of 8–10, 20–30 gallon tanks, small food. The threadfin and blue-eyes are technically a sister family but are sold and kept as rainbowfish.
  • Medium Melanotaenia — the classic community rainbowfish at 3–4.5 inches: boesemani, turquoise (Lake Kutubu), neon-blue, celebes (Marosatherina). Schools of 6–8 in 40–55 gallons.
  • Large Melanotaenia and Glossolepis — Australian, splendid (eastern), parkinson's and the red rainbowfish, 4–6 inches, 55–75 gallon tanks. The hardiest of all types of rainbow fish.

Medium Types of Rainbow Fish: Boesemani, Turquoise and Celebes

The boesemani rainbowfish is the most popular of all types of rainbow fish and for good reason: hardy, peaceful, 4 inches, and the only common freshwater fish that is two colours at once — blue-purple at the front, orange-yellow at the back. It takes a year to colour up and lives ten. The full profile is on the boesemani rainbowfish care page. The turquoise rainbowfish from Lake Kutubu is the same size and temperament in electric blue-green, arguably the most intense blue in freshwater, and the two species are the classic pair for a 75-gallon tank. The neon-blue is a smaller, steel-blue Kutubu relative. The celebes rainbowfish from Sulawesi is a different family, more delicate, at 3 inches with yellow fins and a blue lateral line; it needs small food, very stable water and a calmer tank than Melanotaenia.

Large Types of Rainbow Fish: Australian, Splendid and Red

The large species are the toughest fish in the family and the best choice for a 4-foot tank in a hard-water area. The Australian rainbowfish (M. fluviatilis, and its close relative the crimson-spotted M. duboulayi) tolerates the widest temperature range of any rainbowfish, from 68 to 82°F, and lives 6–8 years. The splendid or eastern rainbowfish comes in a dozen regional colour forms with chequered yellow, red and blue. Parkinson's males develop a glowing orange rear half. The red rainbowfish (Glossolepis incisus) from Lake Sentani is the largest at 5–6 inches and the only one where a dominant male is solid red from nose to tail; the females are olive, and the red only appears in a school with two or more males competing. All four need 55–75 gallons and 4 feet. Profiles: Australian rainbowfish.

Dwarf Rainbowfish: Dwarf Neon, Threadfin and Blue-Eyes

The dwarf neon rainbowfish (M. praecox) is the small tank's rainbowfish: 2–2.5 inches, violet-blue with red (male) or yellow (female) fins, 8–10 in a 20-gallon long. It runs warmer than the rest of the genus and ships badly, so it goes into a mature tank after quarantine; profile on the dwarf neon rainbowfish care page. The threadfin rainbowfish is the most beautiful of the tiny species, with black, thread-like trailing fins on the males, and the most demanding: 1.5 inches, powdered or live food only, no boisterous tank mates, and a short life of three to four years. The forktail blue-eye and Gertrude's spotted blue-eye (Pseudomugil) are 1.5–2 inch nano-tank fish with luminous blue eyes and yellow or spotted fins, best in species tanks of ten or more with floating plants. Profile: threadfin rainbowfish.

Best Rainbow Fish for Beginners and for Colour

  1. Boesemani — hardy, 40 gallons, the two-tone colour, ten-year lifespan. The default first rainbowfish.
  2. Australian rainbowfish — the toughest, widest temperature range, 55 gallons.
  3. Turquoise rainbowfish — as hardy as boesemani and the best blue; 40–55 gallons.
  4. Dwarf neon — the pick for a 20-gallon long, once the tank is mature.
  5. Red rainbowfish — the most dramatic, if you have a 75.

Save the threadfin, celebes and Madagascar rainbowfish for a second tank once the first school has taught you the routine. Whichever type you choose, the colour you are buying only appears in a school of six or more in hard water — the numbers are in the rainbow fish care guide and on the water parameters page, and the adult sizes and lifespans are under rainbow fish size and lifespan.
