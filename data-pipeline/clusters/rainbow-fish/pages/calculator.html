This rainbow fish tank size calculator gives you the minimum gallons and the minimum tank length for a school of the species you want, because rainbowfish are schooling sprinters that are stocked by the group, not by the fish. Choose the species, the number in the school, whether a second species will share the tank and how heavily it is planted, and it returns a tank you can buy — along with the reasoning, so you can see why ten dwarf neons fit a 20-gallon long and six boesemani do not.

How the Rainbow Fish Tank Size Calculator Works

The inch-per-gallon rule fails for rainbowfish twice over: it ignores the school (six 1.5-inch juveniles “fit” a 10-gallon by that rule) and it ignores length. The calculator uses school minimums and a length check instead:

  1. Species minimum for a school of six (eight for dwarf species): 20 gallons and 30 inches for the dwarf band (praecox, threadfin, forktail, Gertrude's), 40 gallons and 36 inches for the medium band (boesemani, turquoise, neon-blue, celebes), 55 gallons and 48 inches for the large band (Australian, splendid, parkinson's), 75 gallons for the red rainbowfish. Adult sizes follow the Melanotaenia genus account.
  2. Group size. Each fish beyond the base school adds 12% of the minimum. Ten boesemani need about 59 gallons; twelve dwarf neons about 30.
  3. A second species adds 70% of its own minimum, since two schools share swimming room but not bioload.
  4. Planting. A heavily planted tank adds 10% for lost swimming room.
  5. Length check. The result is rounded up to the next standard tank that also meets the longest species' minimum length; a tall tank never passes on volume alone.

The reasoning behind the length rule is on the rainbow fish tank size guide, and the species are compared on the types of rainbow fish page.

Two Rainbow Fish Species in One Tank

Set the first species to the larger of the two and the second to the smaller; the calculator adds 70% of the second species' minimum and reports the length for the larger. Two rules it cannot enforce: each species needs its own school of six (eight for dwarfs) — a single of each is not a school — and Melanotaenia species hybridise when groups are too small, so keep the schools full. Pairings that work are on the rainbow fish tank mates page.

Signs the Tank Is Too Small for Your Rainbow Fish

  • Pale colour that never improves in fish over a year old.
  • One male chasing everything all day, rather than dawn sparring across the school.
  • Fish stacked at one end or pacing the same short run of glass.
  • Jumping during the morning chases — a longer tank gives the chased fish somewhere to go.
  • Nitrate that climbs between weekly changes despite the two-minute feeding rule.

If you see two or more, run the calculator again with the adult sizes and full school in mind, and plan the upgrade. Everything else — water, food, health — is in the rainbow fish care guide.
