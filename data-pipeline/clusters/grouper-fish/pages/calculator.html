This grouper fish tank size calculator gives you the minimum gallons and the minimum tank length for the grouper you want, sized for the adult the 3-inch juvenile will become, because that is the only size that matters for a fish that reaches most of its length within two years. Pick the species, add the large tank mates you plan to keep with it and say whether the tank is heavily rock-scaped, and it returns a tank you can buy — along with the reasoning, so you can see why a miniatus needs a 125 and a panther grouper a 180.

How the Grouper Fish Tank Size Calculator Works

Groupers are stocked one to a tank and judged by body volume and bioload rather than swimming room, so the calculator does not use an inch-per-gallon rule (which would put a 14-inch grouper in a 14-gallon tank). It uses species bases and three adjustments:

  1. Species base. The adult length sets a base tank: 75 gallons and 4 feet for the 8–10 inch hinds, 100–125 gallons and 5 feet for the 12–16 inch coral groupers, 180 gallons and 6 feet for panther and bluespotted groupers, 300 gallons and 8 feet for lyretail and red groupers. Adult sizes follow the grouper family account.
  2. Large tank mates. Each big companion (a lionfish, a large tang, an eel, a trigger) adds 25% of the base, for the extra bioload and the extra body in the water.
  3. Rockwork. A heavily scaped tank adds 15%, because rock removes the open striking lane and the grouper needs a cave it fits.
  4. Length check. The result is rounded up to the next standard tank that also meets the species' minimum length — and, for fish over 16 inches, a width of 24 inches or more.

The reasoning behind the bases, and the answers for 55, 75, 125 and 180 gallon tanks, are on the grouper fish tank size guide; the species are compared on the types of grouper fish page.

Using the Calculator With Tank Mates

Count only the large fish — anything half the grouper's length or more, which is the only kind of tank mate that survives (see grouper fish tank mates). Small fish are not counted because they are food. A miniatus with a lionfish returns 180 gallons and with a lionfish and a naso tang 240; a panther grouper with a lionfish, a tang and an eel returns 400. For two groupers, set the species to the larger and add the second as a tank mate, and the calculator enforces a 300-gallon floor.

Signs the Tank Is Too Small for Your Grouper

  • The grouper cannot turn without touching the glass or the rock.
  • Nitrate climbs past 30 ppm between weekly water changes despite feeding only twice a week.
  • Pits around the eyes (HLLE) and a fish that stays pale — chronic stress. See grouper fish diseases.
  • Constant circling near the surface instead of resting in a cave; there is no cave it fits.
  • Chasing every tank mate all day: the whole tank is now its territory.
