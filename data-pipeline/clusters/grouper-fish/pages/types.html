There are about 160 grouper species, but the types of grouper fish that belong in a home aquarium come down to a dozen, in three size bands: the dwarf hinds at 8–12 inches (blue-line hind, V-tail, bluespotted hind), the medium coral groupers at 12–16 inches (miniatus, leopard hind, harlequin), and the large species at 20–30 inches (panther, bluespotted grouper, lyretail, red). A fourth group — bumblebee, giant, goliath and Nassau groupers — is sold in the same shops at the same 4-inch size and grows to 3–8 feet. The band tells you the tank; the species tells you the colour and temper.

The Three Size Bands of Aquarium Grouper

  • Dwarf hinds (Cephalopholis) — 8–12 inches, 75–100 gallons. Blue-line hind, V-tail grouper, bluespotted hind. Small mouths for groupers, but still predators.
  • Medium coral groupers (Cephalopholis) — 12–16 inches, 100–125 gallons. Miniatus (coral hind), leopard hind, harlequin grouper. The best types of grouper fish for most keepers.
  • Large (Cromileptes, Cephalopholis argus, Variola, small Epinephelus) — 20–30 inches, 180–300 gallons. Panther, bluespotted grouper, lyretail, red grouper. Showpieces for very large tanks.
  • Public aquarium only (Epinephelus giants) — bumblebee, giant, goliath, Nassau, black grouper. 3–8 feet.

Dwarf Hinds: Blue-Line, V-Tail and Bluespotted Hind

The smallest types of grouper fish are the hinds of the genus Cephalopholis that stop at 8–12 inches. The blue-line hind is a chocolate-brown fish with electric blue lines and the mildest temper in the family, happy alone in a 75-gallon tank with a lionfish. The V-tail grouper (flagtail grouper) is red-brown with white bars on the tail and equally manageable. The bluespotted hind grows a little larger and is bolder. All three are wild-caught, ship well, eat frozen food within days and live 10–12 years; they are the groupers for a keeper with a 4-foot tank, and the only ones that can be kept with fish as small as a 4-inch wrasse for any length of time.

Miniatus, Leopard and Harlequin Groupers

The miniatus grouper (coral hind) is the best aquarium grouper for most people: 12–16 inches, red-orange covered in blue spots, hardy, colourful, and content in a 100–125 gallon tank with a cave. It is the species most shops mean by “grouper” and has its own page: miniatus grouper care. The leopard hind is a slightly smaller cousin with the same spots on an orange body and a cream bar on the tail. The harlequin grouper is a deep-water yellow-and-blue-lined fish that costs several times as much and needs the same tank. All three are ambush predators with a mouth that takes a fish half their own length.

Panther, Bluespotted, Lyretail and Red Groupers

The panther grouper (humpback or polka-dot grouper) is the most popular grouper in the trade — a white 3-inch juvenile with black spots and a comically small head — and the most outgrown, at 20–27 inches in a 180-gallon tank. It is also the mildest large grouper and the one most likely to be captive-bred; profile on the panther grouper care page. The bluespotted grouper (C. argus, the peacock hind) is dark with blue spots, reaches 2 feet, and is one of the most aggressive fish in the hobby. The lyretail grouper is arguably the most beautiful — violet-red with blue spots and a yellow-edged lyre tail — and needs a 300-gallon tank at 30 inches. The Atlantic red grouper is similar in size and occasionally sold. None of these four is a fish for a 125.

Types of Grouper Fish to Avoid

The bumblebee grouper is the fish that fills the “my grouper outgrew my tank” posts: a yellow-and-black 4-inch juvenile that is the same species as the giant grouper and reaches 3 feet in three years and 8 feet eventually. The Nassau, black, gag and goliath groupers are the same story. Public aquariums take them in when they can; most cannot. If the shop cannot tell you the scientific name, do not buy the fish. For a first grouper, choose the miniatus, the blue-line hind or the panther, sized to the tank on the tank size page, and read the grouper fish care guide for the routine that applies to all of them.
