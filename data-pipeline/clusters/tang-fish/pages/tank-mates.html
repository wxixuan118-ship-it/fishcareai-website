Choosing tang fish tank mates is easy in one direction and hard in the other. With other families — clownfish, gobies, wrasses, blennies, cardinalfish, dwarf angels — a tang is peaceful and mostly indifferent. With other tangs it is the most territorial fish in the reef hobby, and two tangs of the same shape in a tank under 180 gallons will fight until one stops eating. This page lists what works, what does not, how to keep more than one tang, and whether tangs are safe with corals and shrimp. Species-specific pairings are on the blue tang and yellow tang compatibility hubs.

The pattern: tangs ignore fish that do not look like tangs; territorial behaviour in the family is directed at other surgeonfish sharing a grazing patch, as the Acanthuridae account describes. Almost any peaceful or semi-aggressive reef fish qualifies, which is why a tang is the centrepiece of most community reefs. Run any pairing through the fish compatibility checker for the details.

Tang Fish Tank Mates to Avoid

  • Another tang of the same species or genus in under 180 gallons. Two yellow tangs, or a yellow and a purple (both Zebrasoma), is the classic mistake.
  • Lionfish, groupers and large eels with small tangs — a 4-inch tang is food.
  • Triggerfish and large puffers in tanks under 180 gallons; they bully and nip tangs.
  • Aggressive dottybacks or maroon clownfish with a newly added, timid tang in a small tank.
  • Any fish with ich. The most dangerous tang fish tank mate is an un-quarantined one; the tang shows the spots first. See tang fish diseases.

Keeping Two or More Tangs Together

Tangs fight tangs because they compete for the same grazing territory and read a similar body outline as a rival. The rules that let keepers run three or four tangs in one tank:

  1. Different genera, different shapes. A tall Zebrasoma (yellow), a round bristletooth (kole) and an oval Acanthurus (blue tang) coexist far better than two of any one group.
  2. Space. Two tangs need 125 gallons and 6 feet; three need 180; a shoal of the same species (yellow or convict tangs, which shoal on the reef) needs 300 gallons and 8 feet, added together as juveniles.
  3. Add them on the same day, after quarantine, with the lights off, so no fish owns the tank first. If that is impossible, add the boldest species last.
  4. Order of boldness (mildest first): kole and tomini, convict, yellow, blue, scopas, sailfin, naso, powder blue, purple, achilles, clown, sohal. A sohal or purple tang goes in last or not at all.
  5. Feed at two ends of the tank so the dominant tang cannot guard the clip.

Chasing and scalpel displays in the first two days are normal; a tang that is still being chased on day five, or is hiding and not eating, needs to be separated with an acclimation box or a divider before it gets ich from the stress. The signals are decoded on the tang fish behavior page.

Order of Introduction

Add the community in this order: peaceful shoalers and bottom fish first (cardinals, chromis, gobies, clownfish), then dwarf angels and wrasses, then the tang, then any second tang on the same day as the first. A tang added to an established, calm tank settles within a week; a tang added first then treats every newcomer as an intruder, especially in tanks under 6 feet — see tang fish tank size.

Are Tangs Reef Safe?

Yes. Tangs are herbivores that graze algae from rock and glass and leave stony corals, soft corals, clams and shrimp alone, which is why they are stocked in almost every reef tank. Two exceptions worth knowing: an underfed tang may nip at fleshy LPS corals, zoanthids or the mantle of a clam, and naso tangs sometimes pick at soft corals. Daily nori removes the motive. Cleaner shrimp and peppermint shrimp are safe with every tang; a tang will even visit a cleaner shrimp station. Details on feeding are on the tang fish food page.

Tangs and Clownfish

The question behind most tang fish tank mates searches is “can Dory live with Nemo”, and the answer is yes, in a 125-gallon tank. A clownfish pair and a blue tang occupy different water — the clowns stay near their anemone or a corner, the tang cruises the open lanes — and neither looks like a rival to the other. Add the clownfish first. The full pairing, with 90 other partners, is on the blue tang tank mates hub, and clownfish husbandry is in the clownfish care guide.
