Why Tank Mate Selection Is Challenging for Discus

The primary challenge is temperature. Discus require 82–88°F water. The vast majority of common freshwater aquarium fish are kept at 76–78°F, which is too cool for discus to thrive long-term. This single requirement eliminates most of the popular community fish options immediately.

Secondary challenges include feeding: discus are deliberate feeders that take time to approach food. Fast, aggressive feeders will steal every meal before discus can eat, leading to malnourishment even in an apparently well-fed tank.

The safest approach: A species-only discus tank (discus + compatible bottom feeders) is easier to manage and more predictable than a mixed community. Add tank mates only once the discus group is stable and well-fed.

Cardinal Tetras: The Classic Discus Companion

Cardinal tetras are the most popular and reliable discus tank mate for good reason. Both species are native to the warm, soft, acidic blackwater rivers of the Amazon basin and share almost identical temperature and water chemistry preferences. A school of 15–20 cardinal tetras fills out the mid-water layer, adds visual interest, and provides no competition or threat to discus.

Keep cardinal tetras in schools of at least 10. They feel more secure in larger groups and are more active, which benefits the overall display. Cardinals are a bit delicate when first introduced — quarantine new tetras for two to three weeks before adding them to a discus tank.

Corydoras Sterbai: Best Bottom Dweller

Most corydoras prefer temperatures of 72–77°F and are unsuitable for discus tanks. Corydoras sterbai is the exception — it naturally inhabits warm tropical waters and tolerates 82–86°F without stress. A group of six sterbai provides bottom-feeding activity, helps process uneaten food, and is gentle enough not to disturb discus at rest.

Avoid other corydoras species (panda, bronze, peppered) with discus — they will be chronically stressed at discus temperatures even if they survive short-term.

Setting Up a Community Discus Tank

Add tank mates only after the discus group has been established and is feeding well for at least 4–6 weeks. Introduce tetras or corydoras after the discus are settled — not simultaneously. Feed discus separately if needed (turn off the light to slow tetras, then feed discus at their spot) to ensure every fish gets food.

Frequently Asked Questions

Fish that tolerate 82–86°F water and are peaceful feeders include cardinal tetras, rummy-nose tetras, corydoras sterbai, German blue rams, and otocinclus. These species share Amazon habitat preferences and match the discus temperature window.
Angelfish and discus can coexist but the combination is risky. Angels can become aggressive toward discus, especially during breeding, and angels prefer slightly cooler water (78–82°F). Many experienced keepers recommend keeping them separately to avoid chronic stress.
Neon tetras prefer 72–78°F and are not reliably compatible with discus at 82°F+. Cardinal tetras are the correct choice — they are closely related to neons but are native to the warm Amazon blackwater and tolerate 82–86°F well.
Adult discus will eat most shrimp. Small shrimp like cherry shrimp or amano shrimp are generally not compatible — they are a food source in a discus tank. Large filter-feeding shrimp (like bamboo shrimp) may survive if they have refuges, but success varies.
