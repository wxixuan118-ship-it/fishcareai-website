Green Spotted Puffer Care

🟡 6 in  |  🗃️ 30 gal min  |  🧂 SG 1.005 → 1.022  |  ⏳ 10–15 yrs

The green spotted puffer (Dichotomyctere nigroviridis), or GSP, is the most widely sold and most widely mis-kept puffer in the hobby. Shops display 1-inch juveniles in freshwater community tanks. The fish reaches 6 inches, becomes progressively more aggressive, and needs to finish its life in high brackish to near-marine water. Get that right and it is a fifteen-year fish with enormous character.

The Salinity Ramp

This is the whole game with GSPs. In the wild the fish is born in near-fresh river water and migrates seawards as it grows. Reproduce that:

SizeSpecific gravityRough marine salt per 10 gal
Under 2 in (juvenile)1.005–1.0082.5–4 oz
2–4 in (sub-adult)1.010–1.0155–7.5 oz
Over 4 in (adult)1.018–1.0229–11 oz

Raise salinity slowly — about 0.002 SG per week — and remember the biofilter re-adapts each time. Measure with a refractometer, not a floating hydrometer. Full method on the water parameters page.

A GSP kept permanently in freshwater typically shows dull colour, bloating, fungal and bacterial infections, and dies at three to five years instead of ten to fifteen. This is the most common preventable puffer death in the hobby.

Green Spotted Puffer Tank Size

Thirty gallons is the minimum for one adult GSP, and 40 to 55 gallons is the realistic target given the waste load. Use a 36-inch or longer footprint. Two GSPs need at least 55 gallons plus dense hardscape, and many pairs still have to be separated eventually.

  • Filtration: 6–8× turnover, oversized mechanical stage. A canister or sump.
  • Substrate: aragonite sand or crushed coral helps hold pH and alkalinity at high salinity.
  • Hardscape: rock, wood and caves — adults need somewhere to retreat and something to explore, or they get destructive.
  • Plants: at adult salinity, use macroalgae such as Caulerpa rather than freshwater plants.

Green Spotted Puffer Food

Snails and shellfish, always in shell. GSPs have powerful beaks and need the work:

  • Staples: ramshorn and bladder snails, small clams, cockles, mussel, unpeeled shrimp.
  • Supplements: krill, mysis, chopped squid and white fish, frozen bloodworms for juveniles.
  • Frequency: daily under 2 inches, every other day as an adult.
  • Remove leftovers within an hour — shellfish fouls water fast.

Green Spotted Puffer Tank Mates

An adult GSP is effectively a single-specimen fish. Juveniles tolerate company; adults kill it. Where people succeed it is usually with:

  • Other GSPs in 55 gallons or more, introduced young and at the same size, with plenty of visual breaks — and a backup tank ready.
  • Brackish-tolerant, fast species such as knight gobies, monos or scats in large tanks, with mixed results.
  • Nothing else. No shrimp, no ornamental snails, no long-finned fish, and no freshwater community fish, which cannot survive the salinity anyway.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size tank does a green spotted puffer need?

Thirty gallons minimum for one adult, with 40 to 55 gallons preferred and a 36 inch or longer footprint. Two green spotted puffers need at least 55 gallons plus heavy hardscape, and they still often have to be separated.

Do green spotted puffers need salt water?

They need brackish water that increases with age. Juveniles under 2 inches sit at specific gravity 1.005 to 1.008, sub-adults at 1.010 to 1.015, and adults over 4 inches at 1.018 to 1.022, which is close to full marine.

Can a green spotted puffer live in freshwater?

Only as a young juvenile, and only temporarily. Kept in freshwater long term, a green spotted puffer loses colour, bloats, becomes prone to fungal and bacterial infection, and typically dies at three to five years rather than ten to fifteen.

What do green spotted puffers eat?

Shell-on food: ramshorn and bladder snails, small clams, cockles, mussel and unpeeled shrimp, supplemented with krill, mysis, squid and white fish. Feed daily as a juvenile and every other day as an adult.

What tank mates can a green spotted puffer have?

Realistically none as an adult. Other green spotted puffers in 55 gallons or more sometimes work if introduced young at the same size, and brackish species like knight gobies, monos and scats occasionally do in large tanks. Shrimp, snails and long-finned fish never do.

How big do green spotted puffers get?

About 6 inches, reached over two to three years. They are usually sold at around one inch, which is why so many end up in tanks that are far too small by the time they mature.
