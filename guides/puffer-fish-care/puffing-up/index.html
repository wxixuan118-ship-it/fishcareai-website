Puffer Fish Puffing Up and Bloating

🎈 Defence, not display  |  🚫 Never net a puffer  |  💨 Air is the danger

Inflating is what puffers are famous for, and it is the behaviour most misunderstood by new keepers. A puffer does not puff up because it is happy or showing off. It is a last-resort defence that costs the fish energy and carries real risk — and a puffer that inflates regularly in your tank is telling you something is wrong.

How Puffing Up Works

A puffer has an extremely elastic stomach diverticulum and skin that can stretch to several times its resting area. Threatened, it gulps water rapidly into that chamber until the body becomes a rigid sphere, often two to three times its normal volume, which makes it too large for a predator's mouth. Species with spines — porcupine puffers and burrfish — erect them at the same time.

  • It is water, not air. A healthy puffer inflates with water and deflates by expelling it through the mouth and gills within seconds.
  • Puffers have no ribs and no pelvic fins, which is what allows the body to expand that far.
  • Inflation is stressful. It takes energy, and repeated inflation is linked to reduced appetite and susceptibility to infection.

Never Make a Puffer Puff Up

Do not net a puffer, and never lift one out of the water. A puffer that inflates in air gulps air instead of water, and air is far harder to expel. A fish with trapped air floats at the surface, cannot right itself, and may not recover. Move puffers in a submerged container or a bag, always keeping the fish underwater.

The same applies to poking, chasing with a net, or deliberately provoking the fish for a photograph. If someone tells you it is harmless, it is not.

If a Puffer Has Swallowed Air

Signs: the fish floats at the surface, often on its side or upside down, and cannot swim down despite trying. Act quickly.

  1. Hold the fish underwater in a container, gently, head slightly down.
  2. Rock it slowly side to side and stroke along the belly from the throat backwards to encourage the air out through the mouth. Many fish release the air within a minute or two.
  3. Lower the water level in the tank afterwards so the fish does not have to fight to reach the bottom, and reduce flow.
  4. Keep the water pristine and dim the tank while it recovers.
  5. If the fish is still trapped after several attempts, contact an aquatic vet — needle aspiration is a veterinary procedure, not a home one.

Treating Constipation in a Puffer

  1. Fast the fish for two to three days. Puffers handle this easily.
  2. Confirm the water is clean — ammonia and nitrite at zero, nitrate under 20 ppm.
  3. Add Epsom salt (magnesium sulphate) at one tablespoon per 5 gallons, dissolved in change water first. This is a muscle relaxant, not aquarium salt.
  4. Hold the temperature at 78–80°F to keep the metabolism moving.
  5. Resume feeding with shelled prey rather than bloodworms — a diet of soft food is a common cause in the first place.

If the swelling persists past a week of this, treat it as an internal problem rather than constipation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do puffer fish puff up?

It is a last-resort defence. The fish gulps water into a highly elastic stomach chamber until its body becomes a rigid sphere two to three times normal volume, too large for a predator to swallow. It is stressful and costly, not a display of contentment.

Is it bad for a puffer fish to puff up?

Yes, repeatedly. Inflation costs energy and is linked to reduced appetite and infection risk. A puffer that inflates often in your tank is signalling stress from a tank mate, handling or poor conditions.

What happens if a puffer fish puffs up with air?

Air is far harder to expel than water, so the fish floats at the surface and cannot swim down. This usually happens when a puffer is lifted out of the water in a net. Hold the fish underwater head slightly down and rock it gently to release the air.

How do you get air out of a puffer fish?

Hold it underwater in a container with the head slightly down, rock it slowly side to side and stroke along the belly from the throat backwards to encourage the air out of the mouth. Lower the tank water level afterwards and reduce flow while it recovers.

Why is my puffer fish bloated?

Overfeeding causes a normal full belly that resolves. Persistent swelling points to constipation, trapped air, internal parasites, dropsy or long-term exposure to the wrong salinity. Fast the fish two to three days and check the water before treating.

Should I net my puffer fish?

No. Netting risks lifting the fish clear of the water, and a puffer that inflates in air can end up with trapped air it cannot expel. Move puffers in a submerged container or bag, keeping the fish underwater at all times.
