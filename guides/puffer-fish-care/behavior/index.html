Puffer Fish Aggression and Behaviour

😠 Territorial  |  ✂️ Fin nippers  |  🏠 Line of sight drives it  |  🧠 Highly intelligent

Puffers are the most behaviourally interesting fish in freshwater and among the least suited to community life. They recognise their keeper, solve problems, hold grudges against particular tank mates, and investigate everything with their mouth — which is where the trouble starts. Understanding what drives puffer aggression is what turns a chaotic tank into a stable one.

Why Puffers Are Aggressive

  • They are predators with poor speed. A puffer cannot chase down prey, so it evolved to investigate, test and ambush. A fin drifting past is a legitimate target.
  • They hold territory. Most species claim a patch and defend it against anything that enters, including much larger fish.
  • Line of sight drives it. In an open tank a puffer can see every other fish all the time, and the constant visual contact escalates.
  • Hunger raises it. An underfed puffer is a far more aggressive puffer.

Puffer Fish Fighting: What to Do

Fighting between puffers is normally a territory or breeding dispute. Work through this order:

  1. Break up line of sight immediately. Add plants, wood, rock and tall cover. This alone resolves most pea puffer disputes.
  2. Check the numbers. Two pea puffers is the worst configuration; three or more spreads aggression. Amazon puffers need five or more.
  3. Check the sex ratio. Two or three females per male for pea puffers. Multiple males in a small tank is a recipe for constant chasing.
  4. Feed more often, in smaller amounts. A well-fed puffer investigates less.
  5. Rearrange the hardscape. Resetting established territories often resets the conflict too.
  6. Separate. If a fish is being pinned, bitten or kept from food, remove one of them. Persistent bullying does not resolve itself.
Bitten fins need clean water, not medication, in the first instance. Raise water change frequency and watch for fungus or red edges. Treat only if infection appears — and at half dose, because puffers are scaleless.

Fin Nipping

Nipping is not usually a fight — it is feeding behaviour. A puffer that finds a trailing fin interesting will keep returning to it. Once a puffer has learned that a particular tank mate is edible, that behaviour does not stop. The only reliable fixes are removing the target fish or removing the puffer. See the tank mates guide for which fish are viable in the first place.

Glass Surfing, Pacing and Boredom

Puffers are intelligent and get visibly bored in bare tanks. Repeated pacing along the front glass usually means one of:

  • Nothing to do. Add caves, wood, leaf litter, and rearrange the layout occasionally.
  • Reflections. A puffer displaying at its own reflection paces constantly. Dim the room side of the tank or add a background.
  • Water quality. Test ammonia, nitrite and nitrate before assuming it is behavioural.
  • Hunger. Check the feeding schedule on the feeding page.

Enrichment works: puffers will hunt snails released at the far end of the tank, dig in sand, and investigate new decor for days.

Handling and Bites

Large puffers bite hard enough to draw blood and break a fingernail. Never hand-feed a fahaka, mbu, congo or adult green spotted puffer — use long tongs. Never net a puffer either: a netted puffer may gulp air, which can leave it floating and unable to right itself. Move puffers in a submerged container. See puffing up and trapped air.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my puffer fish so aggressive?

Puffers are territorial predators that investigate everything with their mouth, and open tanks with constant line of sight escalate that behaviour. Hunger, too few hiding places, the wrong group size and an unbalanced sex ratio all make it worse.

How do I stop my puffer fish from fighting?

Break up line of sight with plants, wood and rock first, then check group size and sex ratio, feed more often in smaller amounts, and rearrange the hardscape to reset territories. If one fish is being pinned or kept from food, separate them.

Why does my puffer fish nip fins?

Fin nipping is feeding behaviour rather than fighting. A trailing fin looks like prey, and once a puffer learns a particular tank mate is edible the habit does not stop. The only reliable fix is separating the two fish.

Is it normal for a puffer fish to rest on the bottom?

Yes. Most puffers rest on the substrate, on leaves or wedged against decor, and burrowing species such as the congo puffer spend most of the day buried. It only signals a problem when combined with clamped fins, pale colour or refusing food.

Why is my puffer fish swimming against the glass?

Usually boredom in a sparse tank, or displaying at its own reflection. Add caves, wood and leaf litter, dim the room side of the tank, and test ammonia, nitrite and nitrate to rule out a water quality problem.

Do puffer fish bite people?

Large species can and do. A fahaka or mbu bite draws blood and can break a fingernail, so always feed with long tongs and never put a hand in the tank at the same end as the fish.
