Puffer Fish Care Guide (Tetraodontidae)

🐡 ~200 species  |  🗃️ 10–500+ gal  |  🌡️ 75–82°F  |  ⏳ 4–20 yrs

Puffer fish (family Tetraodontidae) are the most personality-heavy fish in the hobby and the most commonly mis-sold. The family name means “four teeth” — every true puffer has four fused tooth plates forming a parrot-like beak that never stops growing. Around 200 species are described, roughly 30 of them spend part or all of their lives in fresh or brackish water, and the rest are marine.

That spread is why generic “puffer fish care” advice fails so often. A pea puffer is a 1.4-inch true freshwater fish that lives happily in a 10-gallon tank. A green spotted puffer sold in the same shop tank will die slowly in that water because it needs to finish its life in near-marine salinity. An mbu puffer reaches over two feet and needs a swimming pool of a tank. Identify the species first; everything else follows from it.

Before you buy. Ask the shop for the scientific name, not the trade name. “Freshwater puffer” on a shop label routinely covers brackish species (Dichotomyctere) and giants (Tetraodon lineatus, T. mbu). Use the puffer species table to match the fish to its real requirements.

What Is a Puffer Fish?

Tetraodontidae sits inside the order Tetraodontiformes alongside boxfish, triggerfish and porcupinefish. Puffers share a set of traits no other aquarium fish combines:

  • No scales and no pelvic fins. The skin is bare or covered in small spines. This is the single most important care fact, because it makes puffers sensitive to copper, formalin and full-strength medications — see puffer diseases.
  • A fused four-plate beak that grows for life. Fed soft food only, the beak overgrows and the fish starves. See puffer fish teeth and trimming.
  • Inflation. Puffers gulp water into a highly elastic stomach diverticulum to roughly triple in volume. It is a last-resort defence, not a trick. See puffing up and bloating.
  • Tetrodotoxin. Most species carry TTX, a potent neurotoxin, in skin, liver and gonads. It is acquired from bacteria in their food chain, so aquarium-raised puffers carry far less — but never eat one, and wash hands after tank work if you have open cuts.
  • Pectoral-fin swimming. Puffers hover and reverse using their pectoral fins, which is why they are slow, precise, endlessly curious and completely unable to outrun a fin-nipping tank mate.

Tank Size and Setup

Puffers are heavy waste producers on a meat-based diet, so tank volume is filtration headroom as much as swimming room. The working rule for the small species is 5 gallons per pea puffer after the first 10, and for everything above 3 inches it is at least 30 gallons for the first fish plus most of that again for a second — if a second is possible at all.

  • Filtration: 6–8× tank volume per hour, biased toward mechanical and biological media. Oversize it.
  • Water changes: 30–50% weekly on any puffer tank. Nitrate under 20 ppm.
  • Cover and structure: heavy planting, driftwood and rockwork for small species; open sand and a few big anchors for burrowers and giants.
  • Lid: required. Puffers are curious and will follow food to the surface.

Water Parameters

Most freshwater puffers want 75–82°F (24–28°C), pH 7.0–8.0 and moderately hard water at 8–15 dGH. Ammonia and nitrite must read zero at all times; puffers are scaleless and show ammonia damage faster than almost anything else you can keep.

Brackish species add salinity to that list, and the target changes with age for green spotted puffers. Get the numbers and the mixing method on the puffer water parameters page, then check your own readings with the water parameter checker.

Food and Feeding

Puffers are molluscivores and crustacean specialists. Almost no puffer will accept flake or pellet food, and the ones that nibble at it still need hard-shelled prey to wear their beak down.

  • Staple hard food: pest snails (ramshorn, bladder, Malaysian trumpet), small clams, cockles, mussel, unpeeled shrimp, crayfish pieces.
  • Soft supplements: frozen bloodworms, blackworms, brine shrimp, mysis, krill, silversides for the large species.
  • Frequency: daily for fish under 2 inches, 4–5 times a week for mid-size, 2–3 times a week for adults over 6 inches.
  • Never feed feeder goldfish or rosy reds. They carry thiaminase and parasites and are a common cause of long-term puffer decline.

Tank Mates and Temperament

Assume every puffer is a fin-nipper until proven otherwise, and assume every shrimp and ornamental snail is food. Some species — fahaka, mbu, congo, avocado, adult green spotted — are effectively single-specimen fish. Others tolerate carefully chosen, fast, short-finned company.

Individual pairings are covered in the pea puffer compatibility hub, and the general rules are on the puffer fish tank mates page. Behaviour, chasing and fighting are on the aggression page.

Health and Common Problems

Because puffers have no scales, they absorb medication faster than scaled fish and are damaged by standard doses of copper, formalin and some organophosphates. Dose at half strength unless the label specifically covers scaleless fish, and treat in a bare quarantine tank whenever possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you care for a puffer fish?

Identify the species first, because requirements vary enormously. Give it the right water type (fresh, brackish or marine), a tank sized for the adult fish, 6-8 times turnover filtration and 30-50% weekly water changes, and feed hard-shelled foods such as snails, clams and unpeeled shrimp so the beak wears down. Keep ammonia and nitrite at zero and nitrate under 20 ppm.

What is the easiest puffer fish to keep?

The pea puffer (Carinotetraodon travancoricus) is the easiest for most aquarists. It stays under 1.5 inches, lives in true freshwater, and a trio fits in a 10 gallon planted tank. Its only demanding requirement is live or frozen food, including a steady supply of pest snails.

Are puffer fish poisonous to keep in an aquarium?

Most puffers carry tetrodotoxin in their skin and organs, but it is not released into the water and handling a healthy puffer is not dangerous. The risk is in eating one, or in a puffer dying and being eaten by tank mates. Wash your hands after tank maintenance and never consume aquarium puffers.

Can puffer fish live in freshwater?

Some can. About 30 species are true freshwater fish, including the pea puffer, Amazon puffer, congo puffer, fahaka and mbu. Others such as the green spotted puffer and figure 8 puffer are brackish and decline in plain freshwater even though shops sell them from freshwater tanks.

Do puffer fish need a big tank?

It depends entirely on species. A pea puffer trio needs 10 gallons, a figure 8 puffer needs 15-20 gallons, a green spotted puffer needs 30 gallons, a fahaka puffer needs 125 gallons and an mbu puffer realistically needs 500 gallons or a custom pond.

Why won't my puffer fish eat pellets?

Puffers are molluscivores with a fused beak built for crushing shells, and most simply do not recognise dry food as prey. Even the individuals that will pick at pellets still need hard-shelled food to grind their continuously growing teeth down.

How long do puffer fish live?

Between four and twenty years depending on species. Pea puffers live 4-5 years, figure 8 and green spotted puffers 10-15 years, and fahaka and mbu puffers 10-20 years or more in a large, well-maintained tank.
