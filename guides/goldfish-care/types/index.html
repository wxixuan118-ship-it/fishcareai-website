Types of Goldfish: Fancy Breeds and Varieties

Compare common and fancy goldfish varieties, from fantail and oranda to ranchu and bubble eye, with care differences that matter before buying.

FishCare AI Editorial Team · Updated September 9, 2026 · Editorial policy

Choose for health as well as appearance

Goldfish varieties belong to the same species, Carassius auratus. “Breed” and “variety” usually describe selected shapes and colours, rather than different species. Body and eye modifications can affect mobility, vision and welfare. Watch an individual swim and eat before choosing it.

Fancy goldfish

A broad group with modified bodies or fins, usually slower than single-tail fish. Match companions by feeding speed.

Common goldfish

Streamlined body and single tail. Plan generous swimming length and an adult-sized aquarium or pond.

Shubunkin goldfish

Single-tail goldfish with calico colouring. Active swimmers that suit spacious, protected ponds.

Fantail goldfish

Double tail with a relatively moderate body shape. Check the fish can swim and feed normally.

Oranda goldfish

Recognised by a fleshy head growth called a wen. Watch for growth interfering with sight or feeding; do not trim it yourself.

Ranchu goldfish

Rounded, dorsal-fin-free body and curved back. Needs calm feeding areas and smooth furnishings.

Ryukin goldfish

Deep body with a pronounced shoulder rise. Avoid sharp decor and assess swimming ability individually.

Telescope goldfish

Protruding eyes can make sight and injury risks important. Protect intakes and provide food in predictable places.

Lionhead goldfish

Dorsal-fin-free variety with head growth. Needs gentle conditions and monitoring of feeding access.

Pearlscale goldfish

Rounded body with raised, pearl-like scales. Avoid abrasion and rough handling.

Bubble Eye goldfish

Fluid-filled sacs below the eyes are vulnerable. Use protected intakes and exceptionally smooth surroundings.

Celestial Eye goldfish

Upward-facing eyes and no dorsal fin. Avoid fast companions that take food first.

Veiltail goldfish

Long, flowing paired tail. Provide space without fin-nipping companions or strong current.

Comet and black moor guides

For long-tailed single-tail fish, read the comet goldfish guide. For the black telescope variety, see black moor goldfish care. Black colouring can change with age; eye shape still affects care.

Frequently asked question

Can fancy and common goldfish live together?

Fast single-tail fish can outcompete slower fancies. Similar swimming ability and reliable feeding access matter more than matching colours.
