How Many Angelfish in a 55 Gallon Tank?

A 55 gallon angelfish tank can support a small adult group when it is tall, well filtered and carefully maintained. A conservative plan is one bonded pair or four to six juveniles while they grow. Adult pairs may become territorial, so stocking must include a relocation plan.

Practical stocking answer

  • One adult pair is the safest long-term choice.
  • Four to six juveniles can grow out together with monitoring.
  • Do not assume a gallon-per-inch rule works for tall cichlids.
  • A 75 gallon tank gives a group more territory and stability.

Why dimensions matter

Angelfish use vertical space and need room to turn without damaging fins. A 55 gallon tank that is at least 18–20 inches tall is preferable to a shallow aquarium. Plants, wood and open lanes should divide the visual field without blocking swimming.

Community stocking

If adding tank mates, reduce the number of angels and choose fish that match warm water, are too large to be eaten, and do not nip fins. Corydoras and bristlenose plecos are common bottom choices; medium peaceful tetras may work when introduced carefully.

Maintenance

Test ammonia, nitrite and nitrate weekly, change around 25% of the water, and clean the substrate without disrupting the biological filter. Watch for rapid breathing, clamped fins and one fish being trapped in a corner.

FAQ

How many angel fish in a 55 gallon aquarium?

One adult pair is the most predictable answer. A group requires individual assessment as pairs form.

Is a 55 gallon tank enough for angelfish?

Yes for a pair or carefully managed small group, provided the tank is tall and mature.
