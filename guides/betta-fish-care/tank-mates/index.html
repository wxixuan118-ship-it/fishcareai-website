Betta Fish Tank Mates: What Can Live With a Betta?

🐠 One male only  |  🌿 Dense planting helps  |  📏 15+ gallons recommended for community

Can Betta Fish Live With Other Fish?

The short answer: yes — but with important conditions. Male bettas are territorial and will aggressively attack other male bettas and any fish that resembles one (colourful, long-finned species). With the right tank mates, in a large enough tank with adequate cover, many bettas coexist peacefully with a wide variety of community fish.

The key rule: No two male bettas together — ever. They will fight to the death. One male betta per tank, always.

The success of any betta community setup depends on three factors: the individual betta's temperament (some are aggressive toward everything; others are very relaxed), the tank size (10 gallons minimum; larger is better), and the tank mate species.

Fish to Avoid With Bettas

These species should never be housed with bettas, or require very careful monitoring:

  • Other male bettas: guaranteed fighting; do not attempt under any circumstances
  • Guppies: male guppies' colourful, long-flowing tails trigger betta aggression instinctively; bettas will attack them
  • Fin nippers: tiger barbs, serpae tetras, and some other barb/tetra species will relentlessly nip at betta fins; the betta's long fins make it an especially tempting target
  • Gouramis: same family as bettas; males may trigger territorial aggression; generally not recommended
  • Cichlids: most cichlids are too aggressive; they will bully or injure a betta
  • Goldfish: incompatible water temperature requirements (goldfish prefer cooler water)
  • Cherry shrimp (small neocaridina): high risk of being eaten; larger species like Amano shrimp are safer
Individual variation: Betta temperament varies enormously between individuals. One betta may coexist peacefully with neon tetras for years; another may attack everything in the tank within minutes. Always introduce new tank mates slowly and monitor carefully for 48–72 hours.

Female Betta Sorority Tanks

Multiple female bettas can sometimes be kept together in a "sorority tank" — but this is an advanced technique that is frequently unsuccessful. If attempting a sorority:

  • Use a 20-gallon minimum, heavily planted with dense sight breaks
  • Introduce all females simultaneously — never add a new female to an established group
  • Keep 5–6 females minimum — aggression is spread across the group rather than focused on one victim
  • Monitor closely for a week; some hierarchical chasing is normal, but any fish being cornered or refused food must be removed
  • Have a backup plan (a separate tank) ready for any fish that cannot coexist

Setup Tips for a Betta Community Tank

  • Size matters: the larger the tank, the easier it is to manage betta aggression — 15–20 gallons gives tank mates escape room
  • Dense planting: lots of plants, driftwood, and hiding spots break lines of sight and give the betta a territory to defend without controlling the whole tank
  • Introduce the betta last: add tank mates first so they establish themselves; adding the betta to an occupied tank is better than the reverse
  • Feed at multiple spots: betta food near the surface, sinking food for bottom dwellers; prevents the betta from monopolising feeding
