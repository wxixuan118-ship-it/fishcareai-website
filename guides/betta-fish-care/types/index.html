Betta Fish Types: Tail Shapes, Colours and Patterns

🎣 13 tail types  |  🎨 12 colour patterns  |  🌏 70+ wild species

Almost every betta sold as a pet is one species, Betta splendens, sorted by the hobby into tail shapes, colour patterns and size forms. This guide covers all four axes — including the wild Betta species that are genuinely different fish — and which of those labels actually change how you keep the fish.

Quick answer: Betta types are named on four independent axes: tail shape (veiltail, halfmoon, crowntail, plakat…), colour and pattern (koi, marble, dragon scale…), size and body form (standard, giant, short-body), and species. A "halfmoon koi plakat" is three of those stacked. Only the species axis is scientific — the rest are show-standard categories.

How Betta Types Are Named

Most betta type lists read as one long jumble because they mix categories that are actually independent. Every domesticated betta has one value on each of four axes, and they combine freely:

  • Tail and fin shape — the structure of the caudal, dorsal and anal fins. This is what "halfmoon", "crowntail" and "plakat" describe.
  • Colour and pattern — pigment and iridescence layers. "Koi", "marble", "dragon scale" and "mustard gas" live here.
  • Size and body form — standard, giant, or the deliberately shortened "balloon" body.
  • Species — nearly always Betta splendens, but the genus holds more than 70 described species, some of them regularly kept.

Only the last axis is taxonomy. The other three are hobby and show conventions, formalised by breeder associations for judging, which is why the boundaries between adjacent categories — delta and super delta, halfmoon and over-halfmoon — are conventions rather than sharp lines.

Betta Tail Types

Tail type is the axis most people mean by "betta types". The categories below are the ones you will actually encounter, ordered roughly from the most common in shops to the most specialised.

Veiltail (VT)Long asymmetric caudal that droops downward rather than spreadingThe default pet-shop betta. Genetically dominant, cheap, and not shown competitively

Over-Halfmoon (OHM)Spread beyond 180°Impressive but can become difficult for the fish to hold up
Rosetail / FeathertailExcessive ray branching giving a ruffled, rose-like edge⚠️ Associated with fin collapse and fin biting — see the welfare note below
Crowntail (CT)Webbing between the fin rays is reduced, so rays project as spikesDistinctive; the exposed rays damage more easily
CombtailA milder version — short spikes along the fin margin onlyOften the result of crowntail crossed into other lines

Spade TailCaudal narrowing to a point, like a spadeUncommon in shops

One more label crosses all of these: dumbo or elephant ear, which describes oversized pectoral fins rather than the tail. It combines with any tail type — a "dumbo halfmoon plakat" is a normal construction — and appears in both sexes, so it says nothing about the fish's sex.

Rosetails and welfare. Rosetail and feathertail finnage is the product of pushing ray branching past the point the fish can support. The extra fin mass drags, tires the fish, and is strongly associated with fin biting — the betta damaging its own tail. If you want dramatic finnage without that risk, a halfmoon or halfmoon plakat is the better buy.

Why marble and koi bettas change colour

A marble betta bought deep blue can be half red six months later, and nothing is wrong with it. The marble pattern is understood in the hobby to come from a mobile genetic element that switches pigment expression on and off in patches through the fish's life, which is why marble and koi lines are famously unstable and why a marble is the one betta you cannot photograph once and expect to match forever.

Colour change that is not normal looks different: overall fading with clamped fins, dark horizontal stress stripes, or patches that come with frayed fin edges. Those point at water quality or illness rather than genetics — check parameters first with our water parameter checker.

Dragon scale: check the eyes. The thick metallic scaling that makes dragon-scale bettas striking can continue to grow over the eyes, a condition keepers call diamond eye, progressively clouding vision and sometimes causing blindness. It is not treatable, and it does not affect the fish's ability to feed as much as you would expect — but it is worth knowing before buying, and worth checking a fish's eyes for a cloudy, scaled-over film.

Size and Body Forms

FormTypical adult sizeNotes
Standard B. splendensAround 70 mm (2.8 in) standard lengthThe size Seriously Fish gives for the species; ornamental strains sit broadly here
"King" bettaSlightly above standardA retail label rather than a strain — usually a large plakat
Giant bettaUp to roughly 10 cm (4 in)Selectively bred for size. Needs 10 gallons or more and larger portions
Short-body / balloonStandard length, compressed⚠️ A deliberately bred spinal deformity; associated with swim bladder problems

Giant bettas are worth a caveat of their own: they are often sold young, at a size that looks like a finished adult, into tanks that will be too small within months. If you buy one, plan for the adult fish. Our betta tank size calculator covers the volumes.

Short-body or balloon bettas are the one form we would recommend against buying outright. The compact shape is a shortened, compressed spine, and the swim bladder problems that follow are a consequence of the body plan rather than something husbandry can fix.

Wild Betta Species

The genus Betta contains more than 70 described species, and a handful appear regularly in the hobby. These are not colour variants — they are different fish with different water requirements, temperaments and breeding strategies.

B. smaragdina

B. macrostoma

B. albimarginata30 mm SL · 73–82°FPaternal mouthbrooderTiny blackwater species; orange with white-and-black fin margins

The split that matters most is bubble nesters versus paternal mouthbrooders. Bubble nesters build a surface raft and the male tends the eggs in it; mouthbrooders carry the eggs and fry in the male's mouth for one to two weeks. That determines the breeding setup entirely, and it tends to track other differences — the mouthbrooders in the table are blackwater fish wanting soft, acidic, often cooler water than a shop betta.

Two practical points for any wild species: use the tightest-fitting lid you can find, because they jump far more readily than ornamental bettas, and do not mix species in one tank. Several of these interbreed, and hybrids muddy the small captive populations that keep wild species available at all.

Does the Type Change the Care?

For domesticated B. splendens, far less than the marketing suggests. Every type needs the same 5-gallon minimum, the same 76–81°F, the same cycled tank and the same high-protein carnivore diet. Colour pattern changes nothing at all. What genuinely varies:

TypeWhat changes
Halfmoon, over-halfmoon, veiltailHeavy finnage adds drag. Keep flow gentle, add broad-leaved resting spots near the surface, and remove sharp decor
Rosetail / feathertailAll of the above, plus watch for self-inflicted fin biting — a chewed, notched tail rather than a frayed one
CrowntailExposed fin rays snag easily; silk or live plants only, never plastic
Plakat / HMPKThe most robust option — but faster, often bolder, and a better jumper. A tight lid is not optional
Dragon scaleCheck the eyes periodically for scale overgrowth
Giant10 gallons or more, and larger food portions
Wild speciesSoft acidic water for most (alkaline for mahachaiensis), cooler for macrostoma, tight lid, species tank

The pattern behind that table: the longer and more elaborate the finnage, the more the tank has to be built around the fish's ability to swim. Everything else is cosmetic.

Choosing a Type

  • First betta: a plakat or a veiltail. Both are hardy, widely available, and inexpensive enough that you are not gambling a large sum on your first cycled tank.
  • Maximum display with minimum fin trouble: a halfmoon plakat — the 180° spread without the drag of a full halfmoon.
  • Something unusual: koi and galaxy koi are widely available and genuinely striking, with the caveat that the pattern will move over the fish's life.
  • Avoid for a first fish: rosetail and feathertail (fin biting), balloon and short-body (spinal deformity), and any wild species until you have kept a betta through a full year.

Whichever type you pick, the tank matters more than the fish does. Set it up and cycle it before buying — our betta tank setup guide walks through it — and check the sex of a short-finned fish before adding it anywhere, using our male vs female betta guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many types of betta fish are there?

The hobby recognises around 13 tail types and a dozen or so colour patterns, which combine freely, plus size forms such as giant and plakat. Separately, the genus Betta contains more than 70 described species — but nearly every betta sold as a pet is one species, Betta splendens.

What is the most common type of betta fish?

The veiltail. Its long, downward-drooping tail is genetically dominant, which makes it the cheapest type to produce and the default betta in general pet shops. It is also the one type not shown competitively.

What is a koi betta?

A marble-derived pattern of red, black and white patches resembling koi carp. Because it comes from the marble gene, a koi betta's pattern keeps shifting through its life — the fish you buy will not look the same in a year. Galaxy koi adds an iridescent speckled layer over the same base.

Do different betta types need different care?

Water parameters, tank size and diet are the same across all Betta splendens types. What changes is flow and decor: heavily finned types such as halfmoons and rosetails need gentler current, more resting spots and nothing sharp, while giants need 10 gallons or more. Wild species are the real exception — they need their own water chemistry and a species tank.

What is the rarest betta type?

Among domesticated bettas, true over-halfmoons and stable show-quality colour lines are the hardest to obtain, because both are difficult to breed consistently. Among wild species, Betta macrostoma is the one most often called rare — it is restricted in range, tightly regulated, and expensive.

Which betta type is best for beginners?

A plakat. Short finnage means the fish swims strongly, tires less, snags less and is far less prone to fin biting than the long-finned types. A standard veiltail is the next-best choice and the most widely available.
