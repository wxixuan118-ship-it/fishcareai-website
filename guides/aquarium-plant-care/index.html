Aquarium Plant Care: How to Grow Healthy Aquatic Plants

Learn how to choose, plant and maintain aquarium plants with practical guidance for light, substrate, fertilizer, CO2, trimming and algae control.

Written by FishCare AI Editorial Team · Reviewed by an aquarium care specialist · Updated July 2026

Quick answer: Healthy aquarium plants need species-appropriate light, a stable nutrient supply, clean water movement and enough space for their roots or rhizomes. Start with easy plants, avoid burying rhizomes, trim damaged leaves, and change light or fertilizer gradually when algae appears.

Choose aquarium plants for your tank

Beginner-friendly plants include Anubias, Java fern, Java moss, Cryptocoryne, Vallisneria and many hardy stem plants. Match the plant to the amount of light, available space, water temperature and whether you use nutrient-rich substrate or liquid fertilizer. A plant labelled “low light” still needs consistent light; it simply tolerates less intense lighting than demanding carpeting plants.

Lighting for planted aquariums

Use a consistent schedule, usually around 6–8 hours daily for a new planted tank. Longer or stronger lighting does not automatically produce healthier plants; it can instead feed algae when carbon dioxide and nutrients are limiting. A timer keeps the photoperiod stable. Increase intensity or duration slowly after new growth appears.

Substrate, fertilizer and CO2

Root-feeding plants benefit from nutrient-rich substrate or root tabs. Water-column feeders can use a complete aquarium fertilizer at the label dose. Avoid adding extra fertilizer simply because growth is slow until you check light, carbon availability, water movement and plant condition. CO2 can improve growth, but it is not required for a successful low-tech aquarium and must be introduced carefully around fish and shrimp.

Real tank notes

For a beginner low-tech planted aquarium, start with 6–7 hours of moderate light, gentle circulation, root tabs for heavy root feeders and weekly observation of new leaves. Record algae coverage, plant growth and water tests before changing more than one variable.

Trimming and routine maintenance

  • Remove yellow, melting or heavily algae-covered leaves with clean scissors.
  • Replant healthy stem cuttings and discard the shaded lower portions.
  • Thin floating plants so they do not block all light from the tank.
  • Keep filter flow and surface movement strong enough for fish oxygen exchange.
  • Clean algae from glass and decorations without stripping every surface at once.

How to control algae without harming plants

Algae usually indicates an imbalance between light, nutrients, carbon and maintenance rather than one single “bad” factor. Reduce the light period slightly, remove visible algae, improve circulation, avoid overfeeding and keep water changes consistent. Do not make several large changes at once, because stressed plants can release more decaying material.

Frequently asked questions

Can aquarium plants grow without CO2?

Yes. Many beginner plants grow well in low-tech tanks with stable light, nutrients and water movement. CO2 is an optional growth enhancement, not a requirement for every planted aquarium.

Why are my aquarium plants turning yellow?

Yellow leaves can result from transplant stress, insufficient nutrients, poor light reaching lower leaves or unstable water conditions. Check new growth and change one factor at a time.

How often should aquarium plants be trimmed?

Trim when leaves block light, stems reach the surface or dead growth traps debris. Fast-growing stems may need weekly attention; slow-growing plants may need trimming only monthly.
