Telescope Goldfish Care Guide: Eyes, Tank and Feeding

Learn telescope goldfish care, from protecting delicate eyes and choosing a roomy tank to feeding, water quality, health checks and peaceful companions.

FishCare AI Editorial Team · Content prepared September 9, 2026

What is a telescope goldfish?

A telescope goldfish is a fancy variety of Carassius auratus with protruding eyes and a double tail. In aquarium searches, “telescope fish” usually means this goldfish. It is a freshwater fish, and its eye shape affects the way you should arrange its home and feed it.

Black Moors belong to this group, but telescopes also come in red, white, calico and mixed patterns. Colour does not change the basic care routine. Telescope eyes are not the fluid-filled sacs of bubble-eye goldfish; do not treat a normal, longstanding eye shape as an infection.

How to care for telescope goldfish

Start with a fully cycled, spacious aquarium, gentle circulation and smooth decor. Allow extra clearance around ornaments so the fish can turn without brushing its eyes. A protected filter intake and a predictable feeding spot make daily care easier.

Each day, watch swimming, breathing and feeding before adding food. Check that every fish finds its portion. Remove leftovers and investigate changes from that fish’s normal behaviour. Test ammonia and nitrite immediately if a fish is distressed; both should be undetectable.

Each week, review water tests, remove trapped waste and change enough conditioned water to control nitrate. Match replacement-water temperature. Clean filter media gently without replacing all biological media at once.

Plan for an adult fish

Choose a long, broad tank instead of a narrow decorative container. Our starting planning allowance is around 30 US gallons for one telescope and 40–50 gallons for a compatible pair, with more space as adult size demands. These are planning allowances, not a guarantee that any tank of that volume is suitable.

Prepare for a fish that may approach 20 cm in body length, plus fins, and a commitment of roughly 10–15 years under good care. Individual size and longevity vary. Read the tank and growth guides before buying a juvenile.

Frequently asked questions

Are telescope fish and telescope goldfish the same?

In aquarium care searches, these names usually refer to telescope-eye goldfish, a fancy variety of Carassius auratus.

Are telescope goldfish suitable for beginners?

They require careful feeding and an eye-safe setup. A prepared keeper with a cycled, spacious aquarium can meet those needs; bowls are unsuitable.
