Telescope Goldfish Types, Colours and Butterfly Tails

Compare red, white, calico, panda and butterfly telescope goldfish. Understand colour patterns, tail shapes and how they relate to the black moor variety.

FishCare AI Editorial Team · Content prepared September 9, 2026

Eye shape, colour and tail are separate traits

Telescope describes the protruding eyes. Colour names describe pigmentation, while butterfly describes a tail shape most clearly appreciated from above. A fish can therefore be both calico and butterfly-tailed, with telescope eyes.

These are selectively bred goldfish varieties, not separate species with independent water requirements. Seller labels are not always consistent, so inspect the actual fish and ask about its recent health and feeding.

Red, white, calico and panda telescopes

Red or orange telescopes have warm pigmentation, sometimes with white areas. White telescopes have predominantly pale bodies; a normal white variety should not be confused with a formerly coloured fish that suddenly becomes pale and unwell.

Calico describes a mixed pattern with several colours and often a mottled appearance. Panda usually means a black-and-white pattern. Neither pattern guarantees permanent colour: pigment can change as the fish develops. See colour change and turning white for the difference between a pattern and a symptom.

Butterfly telescope goldfish

Butterfly tails spread broadly when viewed from above. Tail carriage and symmetry vary between individuals and breeding lines. Assess whether the fish swims and rests comfortably rather than selecting only for an extreme outline.

Allow generous clearance around decor and avoid suction or current that traps long fins. A shallow viewing angle does not mean the fish belongs in a small container. It still needs an adult-sized, filtered aquarium.

Where do Black Moors fit?

Black Moors are black telescope-type goldfish. Use the existing Black Moor profile for this variety; a colour-specific duplicate care page is unnecessary.

Whether a telescope is black, red or calico, eye protection and access to meals remain priorities. Compare companions by speed, adult size and behaviour rather than matching colours.

Frequently asked questions

Is a panda telescope a different species?

No. Panda describes a black-and-white pattern in a selectively bred goldfish variety.

Will a panda telescope keep its black markings?

Not necessarily. Pigment can change with development, so the seller cannot guarantee a permanent pattern.
