How to Tell If Angelfish Eggs Are Fertilized

Fertilized angelfish eggs usually remain translucent amber or tan and develop a tiny dark spot or embryo as they mature. Unfertilized eggs often turn opaque white within a day and may grow fungus. Color alone is not perfect, so observe the clutch over time.

Signs to watch

  • Healthy eggs stay clear to amber and show gradual development.
  • White, opaque eggs are often infertile or fungus-affected.
  • Parents fanning eggs and removing damaged ones is normal.
  • Hatching commonly occurs within 24–72 hours.

Conditions for development

Keep temperature stable in the normal angelfish range, ammonia and nitrite at zero, and water gently oxygenated. Strong flow, sudden water changes and poor hygiene can damage a clutch. A separate breeding tank offers more control, but many pairs spawn successfully in a community aquarium.

Should white eggs be removed?

Removing obvious fungus can reduce spread, but it is not essential when parents are actively cleaning the clutch. Use clean tools and avoid scraping healthy eggs. If the entire clutch turns white, review whether the pair is compatible or whether both fish may be female.

FAQ

How can I tell if angel fish eggs are fertilized?

Look for clear or amber eggs and visible development over the first two days. White eggs are commonly unfertilized.

What happens after fertilization?

Embryos develop, hatch into attached larvae, and become free-swimming fry around one week later.
