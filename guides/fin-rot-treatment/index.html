Fin Rot Treatment: What to Use, How Long It Takes and When to Stop

🧪 Fix the water first  |  📸 Photo on day 0, recheck day 3 and day 7  |  💊 Antibiotics only if it keeps spreading

Updated November 17, 2026 · FishCare AI editorial team

Fin rot treatment starts with the water, not the medicine cabinet. Fin rot is a bacterial infection of the fin edges, usually by bacteria such as Aeromonas and Pseudomonas that live in every aquarium and only cause trouble when fish are stressed. The Merck Veterinary Manual links bacterial outbreaks to poor water quality, organic build-up, handling and temperature swings. Fix those, and mild fin rot usually stops spreading within about a week. Medicine is for the cases that keep going.

Treat it as urgent if: the rot reaches the base of the fin or the body, there are red streaks or open sores, white or grey fuzz appears at the mouth, gills or back, or several fish are sick at once. These can be columnaris or a body-wide infection, which can kill within days. Start antibacterial treatment and contact a fish vet if you can.

Is It Fin Rot?

  • Fin rot: ragged, uneven edges that get shorter over days, often with a white, black or red rim. Rays may stick out past the fin tissue.
  • Torn fins: a clean split or notch that appeared suddenly, often from a net, decor or a tank mate. It doesn't creep back towards the body.
  • Fin nipping: neat bites, usually on long-finned fish kept with barbs, tetras or other active fish.
  • Healing tissue: a clear or milky-white edge on a fin that has stopped shrinking. Don't mistake it for fungus.

The key tool is a photo. Take a clear side-on picture of the fish on day 0, and compare every recheck with it. Green rows are the points where you decide what happens next.

    When Fin Rot Needs Antibiotics

    UF/IFAS puts it simply: antibiotics don't cure a fish, they hold back the bacteria long enough for the fish's immune system to finish the job. That only works once the stress that caused the infection is gone. Use them when:

    • the rot is still spreading after about a week of clean, stable water,
    • it reaches the base of the fin or the body, or there are ulcers or red streaks,
    • you see white or grey fuzz at the mouth, gills or back, which can be columnaris, or
    • several fish are affected or dying.

    How to use them well:

    • Treat in a hospital tank. Antibiotics in the water can kill the filter bacteria that process ammonia, so UF/IFAS advises against bath treatment in a tank with a biological filter.
    • Pick one and finish the full course on the label, even if the fins look better halfway. Stopping early helps resistant bacteria survive.
    • Don't combine or keep switching antibiotics. Switching drugs every few days without a diagnosis breeds resistance and often misses the real problem, which may be the water.
    • Ask a fish vet for a culture if the infection keeps coming back. Lab testing is the only way to know which drug the bacteria respond to.

    How Long Fins Take to Grow Back

    DamageRot stopsRegrowth
    Ragged edges onlyWithin about a week of clean water2 to 4 weeks
    Fins shortened by a third or more1 to 2 weeks, often with medicine1 to 3 months; may grow back a slightly different shape
    Rot reached the fin baseNeeds antibacterial treatmentMonths; some fins may not grow back fully

    These are typical ranges from keepers' experience, not measured figures. Young fish and warm, clean water regrow faster.

    When to Stop Treating Fin Rot

    • Any medicine course is finished, exactly as the label says.
    • The rotting edge has not moved for at least a week, compared with your photos.
    • New clear or white tissue is growing at the fin edge.
    • Ammonia and nitrite read 0 and nitrate is under 40 ppm.

    Then keep the water clean. Fin rot that keeps coming back almost always points to the tank, not the fish. The aquarium stocking calculator checks whether the tank is overloaded, and the aquarium log tracks a week of test results.

    Fin Rot Treatment FAQ

    Can fin rot heal on its own?

    Mild fin rot often stops once the water is clean and the cause, such as high ammonia, crowding or fin-nipping tank mates, is removed. It does not heal while the conditions that caused it remain.

    How long does fin rot treatment take?

    With clean water, the rot usually stops spreading within about a week. Regrowth takes much longer: several weeks for small tears and a few months for fins that were badly eaten away.

    When should I use antibiotics for fin rot?

    When the rot keeps advancing after a week of clean water, reaches the base of the fin or the body, or comes with ulcers, red streaks or white fuzz at the mouth or gills. Treat in a hospital tank and finish the full course.

    Is aquarium salt a cure for fin rot?

    No. Salt can support fish that tolerate it, but the cure is clean water and, when needed, the right antibacterial treatment.

    What does fin rot regrowth look like?

    A thin, clear or milky-white edge on the end of the fin. It is new tissue, not fungus. Fungus looks fluffy like cotton wool.

    Why does my betta keep getting fin rot?

    Long-finned bettas tear easily, and small unheated or unfiltered tanks build up ammonia fast. A heated, filtered tank of at least 5 gallons with weekly water changes prevents most repeat cases.
