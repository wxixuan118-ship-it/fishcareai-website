How Big Do Parrot Fish Get? Size and Growth

📏 7–8 in adult  |  📈 ~1 in per 2 months (year 1)  |  🏁 Full size by year 3

Blood parrot cichlids are sold at 1–2 inches and end up as fish the size of a grapefruit. Knowing how big parrot fish get — and how fast — is the difference between buying the right tank once and rehoming a stunted fish in two years. This page covers blood parrot fish size, the growth timeline, and what makes one fish reach 8 inches while another stalls at 4.

How Big Do Parrot Fish Get?

Adult blood parrot cichlids reach 7–8 inches (18–20 cm) in length. Exceptional individuals in large tanks reach 10 inches, and king kong parrot variants can exceed that. Because the body is deep and rounded rather than streamlined, an 8-inch blood parrot looks and stocks like a much larger fish — the body depth is often 5–6 inches.

VarietyTypical adult size
Standard blood parrot7–8 in (18–20 cm)
Large / well-grown specimensUp to 10 in (25 cm)
King kong parrot10–12 in (25–30 cm)
Short-body / balloon parrot4–6 in (10–15 cm)
Marine parrotfish (for comparison)1–4 ft depending on species

Note the last row: if you arrived here after reading that parrot fish grow to four feet, that is the marine parrotfish of coral reefs, a completely different animal. See types of parrot fish.

Growth Rate Timeline

Blood parrots grow quickly for the first year, slow through the second, and are essentially finished by year three.

AgeTypical sizeStage
Hatch to 2 months0.5–1 inFry; drab brown-grey
3–6 months1.5–3 inSale size; colour starts turning orange
6–12 months3–5 inFastest growth phase; needs full-size tank
1–2 years5–7 inBody deepens; adult shape appears
2–3 years7–8 inAdult size reached; growth mostly stops
3+ years8 in+Marginal gains only

A rough working figure is about an inch every two months through the first year, tapering sharply afterwards. A fish that has not grown at all in six months during year one has a husbandry problem, not a genetic one.

What Controls Final Size

  • Tank size. The strongest single factor. A blood parrot in a 20-gallon tank will not reach 8 inches, and the shortfall is not cosmetic — stunted cichlids have internal organs that keep growing against a body that does not.
  • Water quality. Chronic nitrate above 40 ppm suppresses growth hormone response. Weekly water changes do more for size than any food.
  • Diet. Protein-adequate sinking pellets plus frozen foods through the first year. Underfeeding during months 6–12 costs size permanently.
  • Temperature. Growth is metabolic. At 76°F fish grow noticeably slower than at 80–82°F.
  • Competition. A parrot fish outcompeted for food by an oscar or a severum grows slowly no matter what you put in the tank.
  • Genetics. Hybrid stock is variable. Some lines simply top out smaller, and short-body variants are bred to.
Stunting is not "keeping it small". The idea that a fish grows to the size of its tank is a myth in the form it is usually told. Blood parrots in undersized tanks show suppressed external growth with continued internal growth, spinal curvature and dramatically shortened lifespan. Size the tank for the adult from the start — see parrot fish tank size.

Why the Body Shape Matters for Stocking

Standard stocking rules built around slim community fish underestimate blood parrots badly. An 8-inch parrot fish has roughly the body mass of a 12-inch slim-bodied fish and eats accordingly, which is why the tank guidance is 30 gallons for one fish rather than the 20 an "inch per gallon" rule would suggest. Plug your numbers into the tank size calculator rather than counting inches.

Growing Out a Healthy Parrot Fish

  1. Full-size tank by six months. Grow-out in something smaller is fine only for the first few months.
  2. Feed 2–3 small meals daily through year one, dropping to twice daily after that. See the feeding guide.
  3. Hold temperature at 80–82°F during the growth phase.
  4. Change 25–30% weekly without exception; nitrate is the invisible brake on growth.
  5. Keep competition mild so the slow-mouthed parrot actually gets its share.
  6. Measure every few months against the timeline above so a problem shows up early.

Frequently Asked Questions

How big do blood parrot fish get?

Blood parrot cichlids reach 7-8 inches (18-20 cm) as adults, with exceptional fish reaching 10 inches. King kong parrot variants can reach 10-12 inches, while short-body or balloon parrots stay at 4-6 inches.

How fast do blood parrot fish grow?

Roughly one inch every two months through the first year, reaching 3-5 inches by 12 months and 5-7 inches by two years. Growth slows sharply after year two and is essentially finished by year three.

Do parrot fish only grow to the size of their tank?

No. A blood parrot in an undersized tank shows suppressed external growth while its internal organs keep developing, which causes spinal problems and a much shorter life. Size the tank for an 8-inch adult from the start.

Why is my blood parrot fish not growing?

The usual causes are a tank that is too small, nitrate above 40 ppm, underfeeding during the first year, water below 78 degrees Fahrenheit, or a tank mate outcompeting it at feeding time.

How big is a parrot fish in the ocean?

Marine parrotfish, a different animal entirely, range from about 1 foot for princess parrotfish to over 4 feet for the bumphead parrotfish. They are reef grazers and not suitable for home aquariums.
