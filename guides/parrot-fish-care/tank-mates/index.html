🦄 Parrot Fish Care

Blood Parrot Fish Tank Mates

🐠 Calm, similar-sized fish  |  🚫 No nippers or bullies  |  🗃️ 75 gal+ for a community

Blood parrot cichlids sit in an awkward spot: too territorial for a nano community, too timid and too poorly built to hold their own against real cichlid aggression, and big enough to swallow anything under two inches. Good blood parrot fish tank mates are calm, similar-sized fish that neither bully nor nip. This page lists what works, what does not, and settles the two questions everyone asks — oscars and other cichlids.

Understanding Parrot Fish Temperament

A blood parrot will claim a cave, flare at intruders and defend a corner of the tank, especially while spawning. It will also be the fish hiding behind the filter when a Jack Dempsey moves in. The deformed mouth means it cannot lock jaws or deliver a real bite, so its aggression is mostly display — and its defence is mostly running away.

The practical consequence: pick tank mates by behaviour, not just size. A 4-inch convict cichlid is far more dangerous to a blood parrot than a 6-inch silver dollar.

Parrot Fish and Oscar: Can They Live Together?

This is the most-asked blood parrot compatibility question, and the honest answer is sometimes, in a big enough tank, with a close eye on feeding.

The case for it: oscars and blood parrots want the same temperature and pH, and plenty of keepers run the combination successfully for years. The case against it:

  • An oscar reaches 12–14 inches against the parrot's 8, and grows faster.
  • Oscars are aggressive feeders with a normal mouth. A blood parrot with a slit mouth loses every race to the food and can slowly starve in plain sight.
  • Oscars are boisterous. A timid parrot fish that hides permanently is not a fish that is coping.

Minimum requirements if you try it: 125 gallons or more, both fish introduced young at similar sizes, multiple caves and sightline breaks, and target-feeding the parrot fish at the opposite end of the tank. Below 100 gallons, do not. See our fish compatibility checker and the oscar fish care guide.

Parrot Fish and Other Cichlids

Blood parrots are South and Central American in origin, so pair them with New World cichlids that share their water preferences and stay calm:

  • Reliable: severum, keyhole cichlid, other blood parrots, electric blue acara (in a large tank).
  • Workable with space: firemouth, angelfish (watch the fins), rainbow cichlid, geophagus species in 100 gallons plus.
  • Not recommended: convict, Jack Dempsey, green terror, red devil, jaguar, flowerhorn, all African mbuna.

African cichlids are the mistake to avoid outright — they need harder, more alkaline water, and mbuna aggression is far more than a blood parrot can absorb.

How to Introduce New Tank Mates

  1. Quarantine for 2–4 weeks in a separate tank. Every disease that reaches an established tank arrives on a new fish.
  2. Rearrange the decor before adding anyone. Breaking up existing territories resets the aggression map.
  3. Add at lights-out so the new fish gets a night to find hiding spots before it is noticed.
  4. Add in groups where the species is a shoaler, so aggression is spread rather than focused on one fish.
  5. Watch feeding time for a week. Chasing at the food bowl is the earliest reliable sign a pairing will not work.
  6. Have a plan B — a spare tank or a divider — before you introduce anything semi-aggressive.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best tank mates for blood parrot cichlids?

Severums, silver dollars, Congo tetras, giant danios, bristlenose plecos, larger corydoras, rainbowfish and other blood parrots. The rule is calm fish of similar size that will neither bully a timid parrot fish nor fit in its mouth.

Can blood parrot fish live with oscars?

It can work in 125 gallons or more with both fish raised together from a young age, but it is risky. Oscars reach 12-14 inches, grow faster and outcompete a blood parrot at feeding time because the parrot's deformed mouth makes it a slow eater.

Are blood parrot fish aggressive to other fish?

They are semi-aggressive but mostly bluff. A blood parrot will defend a cave and chase intruders, especially when spawning, but its deformed mouth cannot deliver a serious bite and it is more often the victim than the aggressor.

Can parrot fish live with African cichlids?

No. African mbuna need harder, more alkaline water than blood parrots prefer, and their aggression level is far beyond what a slow, poorly armed hybrid can cope with.

Can I keep just one blood parrot fish?

Yes. Blood parrots do not need a group and a single fish in a 30-gallon tank does fine, often becoming noticeably interactive with its owner. They also do well in pairs or groups given enough space and caves.

Will blood parrot fish eat neon tetras?

Yes, eventually. Anything under about two inches is a potential meal. Keep tank mates at three inches or larger, or expect to lose small fish one at a time.
