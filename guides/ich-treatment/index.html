Ich Treatment: What to Use, How Long It Takes and When to Stop

💊 Dose daily at 75–79°F  |  ⏳ 3–5 treatments minimum  |  ✅ Stop only when new spots and deaths have stopped

Updated November 17, 2026 · FishCare AI editorial team

Ich treatment only works on one stage of the parasite. White spot (Ichthyophthirius multifiliis) hides under the skin while it feeds, and inside a cyst on the glass and gravel while it multiplies. The only stage medicine can kill is the free-swimming one that leaves the cyst to find a fish. That is why one dose never works. You need repeated doses, timed to the water temperature, until new parasites stop hatching. At 75–79°F that usually means daily treatment for 7 to 14 days.

Ich is an emergency. One parasite can produce up to 1,024 offspring in a single cycle, and untreated outbreaks can kill every fish in the tank. Start treatment the day you see spots. If fish gasp, flash hard or stop eating, start now and confirm later.

Day-by-Day Ich Treatment Schedule

This schedule assumes a tropical tank at 75–79°F and a medication you dose once a day, or a salt bath. Follow the dose on the label of the product you use. Green rows are the points where you stop and look.

DayWhat to doWhat to expect
Day 0Test ammonia, nitrite and temperature. Take carbon out of the filter. Add an air stone. Vacuum the gravel, then give the first dose.Spots look like grains of salt on the body and fins. Fish may flash against decor or breathe fast.
Days 1–2Dose every 24 hours. Change 25–30% of the water and vacuum the gravel every other day, before the next dose.Spots may increase. Those parasites were already under the skin and can't be reached yet. This is normal.
Day 3 · recheckCount the spots on the worst fish. Check breathing and appetite. Keep dosing.The first spots should be dropping off. If fish gasp at the surface, do a large water change and add aeration before the next dose.
Days 4–6Keep dosing daily and cleaning every other day. Remove any dead fish straight away.Fewer new spots. Old spots fall off and the skin may look dull for a few days.
Day 7 · recheckAre new spots still appearing? If yes, keep going. If nothing has changed at all, recheck the diagnosis.Most tanks are close to clear. A salt bath can end between day 7 and day 10 if spots have stopped.
Day 10 · stop checkStop when no new spots have appeared for 3 days and no fish have died. Otherwise continue and check again in 3 days.Clear skin, normal breathing and feeding.
AfterChange 30–50% of the water, put fresh carbon back and bring the temperature down slowly. Watch the fish for 2 weeks.Weakened fish can pick up a bacterial infection afterwards, and survivors can carry low levels of ich.

    How Temperature Changes the Ich Schedule

    Temperature sets the speed of the life cycle, and the dosing interval has to match it. UF/IFAS gives these intervals:

    Water temperatureLife cycleHow often to treatMinimum
    75–79°F (24–26°C)About 3 to 6 daysEvery day3 to 5 treatments
    Around 60°F (16°C)Much slowerEvery 3 to 5 days5 treatments

    Many keepers raise a tropical tank to about 80°F during treatment to shorten the cycle. If you do, raise it by 1–2°F a day, stay within your fish's range and add aeration, because warm water holds less oxygen. Goldfish and other coldwater fish should be treated at their normal temperature on the slower schedule.

    Ich Treatment Options Compared

    TreatmentHow it is usedWatch out for
    Formalin productsUF/IFAS gives 25 mg/L (about 1 ml of formalin per 10 gallons), with a half dose of 12.5 mg/L for sensitive species. Many ich medicines combine formalin with malachite green.Lowers oxygen, so aerate hard. If fish look stressed or try to jump, change the water at once.
    Salt bath4–5 g/L (about 15–19 g per US gallon) for 7 to 10 days at 75–79°F. Weigh it rather than measuring by spoon.Only for species that tolerate it. Many plants won't, and some catfish, loaches and soft-water fish are sensitive.
    CopperEffective, but UF/IFAS warns it is very toxic to fish when alkalinity is low. Do not use it without testing alkalinity.Kills shrimp and snails. Easy to overdose.
    Potassium permanganateNot recommended for ich.Ich needs repeated doses, and this strong oxidiser can damage skin, gills and eyes if used more than once a week.

    Scaleless fish such as loaches, corydoras and plecos, and many tetras, are often given a half dose. Check the label. Shrimp, snails and other invertebrates should come out of the tank before you use most ich medicines.

    When to Stop Ich Treatment

    UF/IFAS is clear that treatment should not stop until all deaths from ich have stopped. In practice, stop when all three are true:

    • No new white spots have appeared on any fish for at least 3 days.
    • No fish has died for several days.
    • The fish are breathing normally and eating.

    Stopping as soon as the spots vanish is the most common reason ich comes back. The spots disappear when the parasites leave the fish to multiply, which is exactly when the next wave is being made.

    Why Ich Comes Back

    • Treatment stopped too early, before the last cysts in the tank hatched.
    • Carbon left in the filter, which removes many medications.
    • A new fish without quarantine. UF/IFAS recommends at least 30 days, because carriers often show ich 1 to 3 weeks after shipping.
    • Shared nets and siphons. The cysts are sticky and travel between tanks on equipment.
    • Temperature swings and crowding, which weaken the fish's defences.

    White spots are not always ich. Breeding tubercles on male goldfish, lymphocystis and some bacterial infections can look similar. If spots don't change at all after a week of correct treatment, check the symptoms in the fish health library or ask a vet to look at a skin scrape under a microscope.

    Ich Treatment FAQ

    How long does ich treatment take?

    At 75–79°F the ich life cycle takes about 3 to 6 days, so most outbreaks need daily treatment for 7 to 14 days. In cooler water the cycle is slower and treatment takes longer.

    Why do the white spots get worse after I start treating?

    Medication only kills the free-swimming stage in the water. Parasites already under the skin are protected and keep growing, so spots can look worse for the first 2 to 3 days.

    Can I treat ich with salt alone?

    UF/IFAS lists a 4–5 g/L salt bath for 7 to 10 days at 75–79°F as effective in small systems, but only for fish and plants that tolerate that salinity. Check your species first.

    Can I treat ich with heat alone?

    Raising the temperature speeds up the life cycle so treatment works faster, but on its own it is not a reliable cure. Raise it slowly, stay within your species' range and add aeration.

    Is ich medication safe for shrimp and snails?

    Many ich medicines contain copper, formalin or malachite green, which can kill shrimp and snails. Move invertebrates out or treat the fish in a separate tank.

    When can I stop ich treatment?

    Not before the deaths have stopped. A common margin is to keep treating until no new spots have appeared for 3 days, then do a water change and put carbon back.
