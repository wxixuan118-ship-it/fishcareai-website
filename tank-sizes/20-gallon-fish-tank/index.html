20 Gallon Fish Tank: Dimensions, Weight, Stocking & Best Fish

A 20 gallon fish tank comes in two standard shapes: the 20 high at about 24 × 12 × 16 inches (61 × 30 × 41 cm) and the 20 long at about 30 × 12 × 12 inches (76 × 30 × 30 cm). Either weighs roughly 25 lb (11 kg) empty and about 225 lb (102 kg) once filled with water, gravel and décor, and comfortably holds 10–15 small community fish. Both hold about 76 liters.

The 20 is the size where a first community becomes possible — one school, one centerpiece and a bottom group — and where the shape you buy starts to matter. The 20 long gives fast swimmers six extra inches of run and a bigger floor; the 20 high gives taller fish and taller plants more room. For most beginners the 20 long is the more forgiving choice, and it is the shape the species calculators on this site assume for active fish.

20 high vs 20 long: which one to buy

Both tanks hold about 20 gallons, but the 20 long trades 4 inches of height for 6 inches of length and a 25% larger floor (360 versus 288 square inches). That matters for nearly every popular fish. Zebra danios, dwarf rainbowfish and swordtails are lane swimmers — the species calculators set their minimum at a 20 long, not a 20 high. Corydoras, kuhli loaches and shrimp live on the floor. And a larger water surface means better gas exchange for the same volume.

The 20 high wins for tall-bodied fish and vertical layouts: fancy goldfish, a betta with tall stem plants, juvenile angelfish being grown on, or a rimless 60H-style aquascape. If you are not sure, buy the long — it suits more of the fish people actually want in a 20. The 29 gallon keeps the 20 long's 30-inch footprint and adds 6 inches of height.

How Much Does a 20 Gallon Fish Tank Weigh?

Freshwater weighs about 8.34 lb per US gallon (3.78 kg per gallon), so 20 gallons of water would be 167 lb. A running tank holds a little less water than its label, but adds glass, gravel, rock and equipment — and the total is what the stand has to carry.

Call it about 225 lb — roughly the weight of a heavy adult plus a suitcase, concentrated on a 24 × 12 or 30 × 12 inch base. The 20 long comes in a few pounds heavier because its larger floor takes more gravel. A bare-bottom tank comes in near 185 lb; a planted tank with deep substrate and large stones can approach 250 lb.

Placement safety: at about 225 lb, a 20 gallon is past what most desks and flat-pack furniture are designed for. Use a stand built for the exact footprint — a 20 high stand does not fit a 20 long — or very solid furniture that supports the whole base and sits level. Ground floors are not a concern at this weight; on upper floors, place it near a wall rather than mid-span. Keep it out of direct sunlight.

How Many Fish Can Live in a 20 Gallon Tank?

A 20 gallon tank supports roughly 10–15 small community fish — for example one centerpiece fish, one school of six to ten and a small bottom group — or a single-species tank of a fish that suits the shape you bought. That assumes a filter rated for the tank and weekly water changes.

The “one inch per gallon” rule is unreliable here as everywhere: it would allow a single 20-inch fish that could not turn, and it ignores that waste output rises much faster than length. Stock by adult size, behaviour and group needs instead.

What actually sets the limit in 20 gallons

  • Shape. In a 20 high, choose fish that hover or are tall; in a 20 long, active schooling fish become possible. The species table below says which shape each number assumes.
  • Adult size. The 20 is the first size where 3-inch fish work — gouramis, swordtails, mollies — but nothing that grows past about 4 inches.
  • One centerpiece. A betta, a dwarf or honey gourami, or a pair of small dwarf cichlids — not several. Two centerpieces in 20 gallons usually means one is bullied.
  • Schooling minimums. Six is the floor for tetras, rasboras, danios and corydoras; eight to ten looks and behaves better, and the 20 has room for it.
  • Breeding. Mixed-sex livebearers fill a 20 within months. Plan for fry or keep one sex.
  • Maintenance. Weekly 25% changes let you stock toward the top of the range; fortnightly changes mean stocking toward the bottom.

Practical examples for a 20 gallon

  • Works well (20 high or long): 1 honey gourami, 10 harlequin rasboras and 6 panda corydoras — about 17 fish across three levels.
  • Works well (20 long): a school of 10 zebra danios with 6 bronze corydoras below.
  • Works well: 1 male betta, 8 ember tetras and 6 pygmy corydoras in a planted tank.
  • Works, single species: 1 fancy goldfish — the calculator's minimum for the first fancy goldfish is 20 gallons, so there is no room for a second.
  • Does not work: an adult pair of angelfish. They outgrow the height of a 20 high and the depth of both shapes; see the 29 gallon page.

Fish That Are Not Suitable for a 20 Gallon Tank

Most fish that fail in a 20 gallon outgrow it: they are sold at 1–2 inches and reach 5 inches or more. A few others fail because of the shape — fine in a 20 long, cramped in a 20 high. The alternatives below are general ranges, not absolute minimums.

✕

Angelfish (adults)

Juveniles fit; adults reach 6 inches long and 8–10 inches tall, more than either 20 can hold.

29 gallons for a pair, a 40 breeder recommended — see the angelfish tank size calculator.

✕

Single-tail goldfish

Commons, comets and shubunkins reach a foot and produce heavy waste.

40 gallons for the first fish per the goldfish calculator; a pond long term.

✕

Common pleco

Reaches 18–24 inches. Sold as an algae eater for small tanks, outgrows a 20 within a year.

150+ gallons. Use a bristlenose (20 long) or nerite snails instead.

✕

Tiger barb

Needs a group of at least six to keep nipping in check, and that group is too active for 20 gallons.

6–8 in a 29–30 gallon or larger.

✕

Rainbow shark

A 6-inch, territorial bottom fish that claims the whole floor of a 20.

A single fish in a 4-foot tank.

✕

Clown loach

A social fish that reaches 10–12 inches and lives 15+ years.

A group of five or more in 100–125+ gallons.

✕

Silver dollar and bala shark

Both are large schooling fish — 6 inches and 12–14 inches — that need groups and long tanks.

A 6-foot tank.

✕

Oscar

Grows 12–14 inches with a large appetite and waste output.

75 gallons for one oscar — see the oscar tank size calculator.

Shape-dependent: zebra danios, swordtails, dwarf rainbowfish and a bristlenose pleco are fine in a 20 long but cramped in a 20 high. Mollies fit in a group of five, but sailfin mollies need about 40 gallons.

How to Set Up a 20 Gallon Fish Tank

Setting up a 20 gallon fish tank follows the standard freshwater sequence — our how to set up a fish tank guide covers the fundamentals. The steps below add what changes at this size.

  1. Choose the shape and standDecide between the 20 high and 20 long first — stands, lids and lights differ. Use a stand built for the exact footprint, near an outlet and away from windows and heat sources.
  2. Level the empty tankCheck level side to side and front to back before adding anything. Shim the stand, not the tank.
  3. Add substrateAbout 30–40 lb of rinsed gravel or sand for a 2-inch layer. Use sand for corydoras and kuhli loaches; a plant substrate under gravel for rooted plants.
  4. Install filter and heaterA hang-on-back filter rated for 20–40 gallons or a small canister, and a 100 W adjustable heater near the outlet. In a 20 long, put the filter at one end and the heater at the other so warm water crosses the tank.
  5. Add hardscape and plantsStones and wood first, then plants. A 20 high suits tall stems and vertical wood; a 20 long suits a low, wide layout with open swimming space in the middle. See the plant care guide.
  6. Fill and condition the waterFill over a plate to keep the substrate in place, and dose dechlorinator for about 18 gallons. See preparing water for a fish tank.
  7. Cycle the tankEstablish the filter bacteria before adding fish — usually 3–6 weeks. Our how to cycle a fish tank guide and the cycle tracker walk through it.
  8. Confirm water parametersAmmonia and nitrite at zero within 24 hours of dosing, nitrate present. Check pH, GH and KH against your chosen species — see aquarium water parameters.
  9. Add fish in stagesStart with the bottom group or the school, wait two to three weeks, then add the centerpiece. Adding everything at once risks new tank syndrome.
  10. Monitor the first monthsTest weekly for the first two months, and whenever fish behave oddly.

Equipment for a 20 Gallon Aquarium

A 20 gallon takes small-to-mid equipment, and the shape changes a few choices. These are the targets to check whatever brand you buy.

🌀Filter

Aim for four to six times the volume per hour — about 80–120 GPH of rated flow — from a hang-on-back filter labelled for 20–40 gallons or a small canister. Size toward the top for goldfish or livebearer colonies, toward the bottom (or baffle the outlet) for a betta tank. In a 20 long, a single outlet at one end reaches the far end if it is aimed along the front glass.

🌡️Heater

A 20 gallon in a 70°F room heated to 78°F needs about 75–100 W; one adjustable 100 W heater covers most homes. Two 50 W heaters add redundancy. Goldfish and the cool-water danio plan may need none at all. Use a separate thermometer.

💡Lighting

A light must match the length — 24 inches for a 20 high, 30 inches for a 20 long. Low-light plants grow under most stock lights; carpeting plants or red stems need a stronger planted-tank LED. In a 20 high, light must also reach 16 inches down, so a slightly stronger fixture is worth it for plants. Run 6–8 hours on a timer.

🪨Substrate

About 30–40 lb of gravel or sand for a 2-inch layer (more for the 20 long). Sand suits corydoras and loaches; nutrient substrate suits rooted plants.

🪵Stand

A running 20 gallon weighs about 225 lb. Use a stand built for the exact footprint, or very solid furniture that supports the whole base. Do not put a 20 long on a 20 high stand — the ends overhang.

Water Parameters for a 20 Gallon Aquarium

A 20 gallon has no “correct” water chemistry of its own: a betta community wants 78–80°F and soft water, a goldfish tank wants 65–72°F, and a livebearer tank prefers moderately hard, alkaline water. Pick species whose ranges overlap with your tap water.

What the 20 adds over a 10 is stability. Twice the water dilutes an overfeeding twice as much and holds its temperature through a short heater failure, which is why it is the size many keepers recommend for a first tank. Ammonia and nitrite at zero, and nitrate kept low through water changes, are the universal targets.

Test GH, KH and pH from the tap with a liquid kit before choosing fish. Our fish tank water guide and what kind of water for a fish tank explain the options, and how to lower nitrates covers the most common problem.

20 Gallon Fish Tank Maintenance

A 20 gallon is quick to maintain with a bucket and siphon. Use this framework and let your nitrate readings adjust it.

  • Partial water changes. 25% weekly (about 4–5 gallons). If nitrate rises above about 20–40 ppm between changes, change more; a lightly stocked planted tank can stretch to every 10 days. The water change schedule tool sets the rhythm from your readings.
  • Substrate cleaning. Vacuum open areas weekly and leave planted areas alone.
  • Filter maintenance. Rinse sponges in old tank water every 3–4 weeks; never all the media at once.
  • Algae. Scrape the front glass weekly and keep the light to 6–8 hours.
  • Testing. Ammonia, nitrite and nitrate weekly for three months; the water parameter checker explains results and the aquarium log tracks them.
  • Top-ups. Replace evaporation with dechlorinated water once or twice a week.

Is a 20 Gallon Fish Tank Right for You?

The 20 gallon is the size many experienced keepers recommend as a first tank: small enough for a bedroom or office, big enough to forgive the mistakes that sink a 10.

✅ Advantages

  • Forgiving. Twice the water of a 10 gallon, with much steadier temperature and chemistry.
  • A real community. One centerpiece, one school and a bottom group fit comfortably.
  • Two shapes. The 20 long suits active fish; the 20 high suits tall fish and vertical layouts.
  • Still compact. About 225 lb running on a 24- or 30-inch footprint.
  • Cheap to equip. A 100 W heater, a mid-size hang-on-back filter and a simple LED.

⚠️ Considerations

  • Shape confusion. Lids, lights and stands differ between high and long; check before buying.
  • Too heavy for light furniture. Needs a proper stand.
  • No large fish. Angelfish, most barbs and anything over about 4 inches need more.
  • 20 high limits active fish. Danios, swordtails and rainbowfish need the long.
  • Easy to outgrow. Many keepers move to a 29 or 40 breeder within a year or two.

Choose a 20 long if you want an active school, corydoras or a first community — it suits more fish. Choose a 20 high if you want a betta centerpiece with tall plants or a single fancy goldfish. Choose a 29 gallon instead if angelfish or two goldfish are on your list. Choose a 10 gallon only if space is tight and you plan a single betta or a nano school.

20 Gallon Fish Tank FAQ

What are the dimensions of a 20 gallon fish tank?

A standard 20 gallon high is about 24 inches long, 12 inches front to back and 16 inches tall (61 × 30 × 41 cm). A 20 gallon long is about 30 × 12 × 12 inches (76 × 30 × 30 cm). Both hold about 76 liters; lids, lights and stands are not interchangeable between them.

What is the difference between a 20 gallon high and a 20 gallon long?

Volume is nearly identical, but the 20 long is 6 inches longer and 4 inches shorter, with a 25% larger floor. The long suits active swimmers such as zebra danios, swordtails and dwarf rainbowfish, plus bottom dwellers; the high suits tall fish, fancy goldfish and tall plants.

How much does a 20 gallon fish tank weigh?

About 22–28 lb (10–13 kg) empty and about 210–240 lb (95–109 kg) filled and running: roughly 25 lb of glass, 150 lb of water (18 actual gallons), 30–40 lb of gravel and some décor and equipment. A typical setup comes to about 225 lb.

How many fish can I put in a 20 gallon tank?

Roughly 10–15 small community fish, for example 1 honey gourami, 10 harlequin rasboras and 6 panda corydoras, assuming a filter rated for the tank and weekly water changes. Adult size, behaviour and the tank's shape matter more than any inches-per-gallon rule.

How many guppies can live in a 20 gallon tank?

About 10–12 adult guppies, which the guppy tank size calculator treats as the recommended size. A 20 long is slightly better than a 20 high because of its larger surface and swimming length. Mixed-sex groups will breed, so plan for fry.

Can I keep a goldfish in a 20 gallon tank?

One fancy goldfish, yes — 20 gallons is the minimum for the first fancy goldfish, with 10 more gallons for each additional fish. Single-tail goldfish such as comets need at least 40 gallons and ideally a pond.

Can angelfish live in a 20 gallon tank?

Only as juveniles. Adult angelfish grow to about 6 inches long and 8–10 inches tall; a pair needs at least a 29 gallon, and a 40 breeder is recommended.

What size heater do I need for a 20 gallon tank?

About 75–100 W; a single adjustable 100 W heater suits most rooms. Two 50 W heaters add redundancy. Goldfish and cool-water tanks may need no heater.

What size filter do I need for a 20 gallon tank?

About 80–120 GPH of rated flow from a hang-on-back filter rated for 20–40 gallons or a small canister. Size up for goldfish or livebearers; baffle the outlet for a betta.

Is a 20 gallon tank good for beginners?

Yes — it is one of the best first-tank sizes. It holds stable conditions much better than a 10 gallon while still fitting in most homes. The 20 long is the more versatile shape for a first community.

How FishCare AI Makes Tank Recommendations

The species, group sizes and stocking combinations on this page are based on the same factors the Aquarium Planner and Fish Compatibility Checker weigh for every species in our database:

  • Adult size — we plan around the fish an animal becomes, not the juvenile sold in stores.
  • Swimming behaviour — cruising, hovering and darting species need different lengths and depths.
  • Territorial requirements — cichlids and some catfish claim floor space that has to exist in the footprint.
  • Schooling needs — shoaling species are counted as groups, never as individuals.
  • Bioload — body mass, diet and messiness drive the filtration and water-change budget.
  • Aquarium footprint — length, width and surface area, not just gallons.
  • Compatibility — temperament, size difference and fin-nipping risk between species.
  • Water requirements — temperature, pH and hardness overlap across every fish in a combination.

For the 20 gallon fish tank specifically, recommendations cover both the 24 × 12 × 16 inch 20 high and the 30 × 12 × 12 inch 20 long, and say which shape each species needs. Species whose adult size or activity outgrows both shapes were excluded. Per-species numbers match the species tank size calculators.
