29 Gallon Fish Tank: Dimensions, Weight, Stocking & Best Fish

A standard 29 gallon fish tank measures about 30 × 12 × 18 inches (76 × 30 × 46 cm), weighs roughly 45 lb (20 kg) empty and about 320 lb (145 kg) once filled with water, gravel and décor, and comfortably holds 15–20 small community fish. It holds about 110 liters on the same 30 × 12 inch footprint as a 20 gallon long — the extra 9 gallons all go into height.

That height is the 29's defining feature. It makes the tank the smallest common size that suits a pair of adult angelfish, a good home for two fancy goldfish, and a natural fit for tall plants. It does not add any swimming length or floor space over a 20 long, so fish that need a longer run or a wider bottom are no better off here than in the smaller tank.

29 Gallon Fish Tank Dimensions

The 29 gallon is a fairly standard tank — most rimmed 29s share the 30 × 12 × 18 inch footprint, and they share lids and lights with the 20 long. Two similar sizes are often confused with it: the 30 gallon, which is longer and shorter, and the 30 breeder, which is much wider.

29 vs 20 long: what the extra height buys

The 29 and the 20 long sit on the same 30 × 12 inch floor; the 29 is simply 6 inches taller. That extra water column is what tall-bodied fish need — adult angelfish reach 8–10 inches from fin tip to fin tip, and the angelfish calculator treats 18 inches of water depth as the requirement. It also gives fancy goldfish more water to dilute their waste, and lets stem plants grow without hitting the surface.

What the 29 does not add is room to swim or a bigger floor. A school of zebra danios has the same 30-inch lane in both, and six corydoras have the same bottom to forage. If your favourite fish are active swimmers, look at the 36-inch 30 gallon or a 40 breeder instead. Taller tanks are also harder to clean — you will need longer arms or tools to reach the substrate at the back.

How Much Does a 29 Gallon Fish Tank Weigh?

Freshwater weighs about 8.34 lb per US gallon (3.78 kg per gallon), so 29 gallons of water would be 242 lb. With the fill line, gravel and décor, a running tank holds about 26 gallons — but the glass, gravel and rock more than make up the difference.

Call it about 320 lb on a 30 × 12 inch base — about 130 lb per square foot, more concentrated than most furniture is built for. A bare-bottom tank comes in near 280 lb; a planted tank with deep substrate and large stones can reach 350 lb.

Placement safety: use a stand built for a 30 × 12 inch aquarium, not household furniture. A tall, narrow tank exaggerates any tilt, so check level in both directions before filling and shim the stand, not the tank. Ground floors are not a concern; on upper floors place it near a wall. How Many Fish Can Live in a 29 Gallon Tank?

A 29 gallon tank supports roughly 15–20 small community fish — for example a centerpiece pair, one or two schools and a bottom group — assuming a filter rated for the tank and weekly water changes. Because the floor and length match a 20 long, the extra capacity is best spent on height: a taller centerpiece fish or a larger school, rather than more bottom dwellers.

What actually sets the limit in 29 gallons

  • Length. Thirty inches is the same lane as a 20 long; active schools do not gain room.
  • Height. Eighteen inches of water suits angelfish, tall gouramis and fancy goldfish.
  • Floor. One bottom group — six to eight corydoras or a few loaches — is the comfortable limit.
  • One centerpiece. An angelfish pair, a pair of kribensis or a pearl gourami trio — choose one.
  • Schooling minimums. Groups of eight to twelve look and behave better than the minimum six.
  • Maintenance. Weekly 25% changes let you stock toward the top of the range.

Practical examples for a 29 gallon

  • Works well: 1 pair of angelfish, 10 rummy-nose tetras and 6 sterbai corydoras.
  • Works well: 1 pair of kribensis, 10 black skirt tetras and a bristlenose pleco.
  • Works well: 2 fancy goldfish with strong filtration and no other fish — the goldfish calculator allows 1–2 at this size.
  • Works well: a betta sorority of 6–7 females in a densely planted tank.
  • Does not work: a pair of angelfish plus neon tetras — adult angelfish eat small tetras. Choose larger schooling fish.

29 Gallon Fish Tank Stocking Ideas

Six stocking plans for a standard 30 × 12 × 18 inch 29 gallon fish tank. They favour fish that use the tank's height, and none repeats the plans on our 10, 20, 50, 55 or 100 gallon pages.

Angelfish Pair Community

The classic 29: tall centerpiece, robust school
Centerpiece1 pair of angelfish
Schooling10 rummy-nose tetras
Bottom6 sterbai corydoras
HardscapeTall plants and vertical wood at the back

Why it works: The 18-inch height fits adult angelfish, rummy-nose tetras are too big to be eaten and school tightly, and sterbai corys handle the warm 78–80°F water angelfish like. Every level is used without crowding.

Watch for: Buy a pair or two juveniles and expect them to pair off; rehome extras before they fight. Avoid neon tetras — adult angelfish eat them.

Kribensis Breeding Pair

A cichlid family you can watch
Centerpiece1 pair of kribensis
Dither8 black skirt tetras
Clean-up1 bristlenose pleco
HardscapeTwo caves at opposite ends, sand, plants

Why it works: Kribensis breed readily and guard their fry in open view. Black skirt tetras are robust enough to share the tank with a guarding pair and act as dither fish that keep the cichlids confident.

Watch for: Corydoras are often harassed by a spawning krib pair — skip them here. Plan for rehoming fry.

Two Fancy Goldfish

Cool water, heavy filtration, no tropicals
Centerpiece2 fancy goldfish
EquipmentFilter rated for 50–60 gallons, no heater
HardscapeSmooth gravel or bare bottom, anubias and java fern

Why it works: The goldfish calculator allows one or two fancies in 29–30 gallons. The extra height over a 20 suits round-bodied varieties, and heavy filtration with weekly changes keeps up with their waste. See the goldfish care guide.

Watch for: Do not add a third goldfish or any tropical fish. Choose smooth décor — fancy goldfish damage their eyes and fins on sharp edges.

Betta Sorority

Six or seven females in a jungle of plants
Centerpiece6–7 female bettas
Bottom6 pygmy corydoras
HardscapeDense planting, floating plants and broken sight lines

Why it works: The betta calculator calls 29 gallons the sweet spot for a stable sorority. Dense plants break sight lines so no female can dominate the whole tank, and pygmy corys stay out of the way on the floor.

Watch for: Add all the females at once and have a spare tank ready for any bully. Sororities are an intermediate project, not a beginner one.

Pearl Gourami Planted Tank

Graceful surface fish over a calm school
Centerpiece1 male + 2 female pearl gouramis
Schooling12 harlequin rasboras
Bottom6 panda corydoras

Why it works: Pearl gouramis use the upper half of the tall water column, harlequins school in the middle and pandas forage below. All are peaceful and thrive in soft, slightly acidic water at 75–78°F.

Watch for: Gouramis need calm water near the surface — aim the filter outlet along the glass. Panda corys prefer the cooler end of the range.

Hard-Water Livebearer Tank

Mollies and swordtails in alkaline tap water
Upper5 mollies
Mid-water4 swordtails (1 male, 3 females)
HardscapeJava moss and floating plants for fry

Why it works: If your tap water is hard and alkaline, livebearers thrive where soft-water species struggle. Mollies and swordtails share the same 72–80°F range and add size and colour to a 29.

Watch for: Both breed constantly; expect to rehome fry. Keep a single male swordtail to avoid fighting.

How to Set Up a 29 Gallon Fish Tank

Setting up a 29 gallon fish tank follows the standard freshwater sequence — our how to set up a fish tank guide covers the fundamentals. The steps below add what changes for a taller tank.

  1. Choose the location and standUse a stand built for a 30 × 12 inch aquarium, near an outlet and away from windows. Leave room above the tank to reach in — an 18-inch depth needs arm room.
  2. Level the empty tankLevel in both directions before filling. A tall, narrow tank magnifies any tilt.
  3. Add substrateAbout 35–45 lb of gravel or sand for a 2-inch layer. Slope it up toward the back for a sense of depth.
  4. Install filter and heaterA hang-on-back filter rated for 30–55 gallons or a small canister, with the intake low and the outlet high so the whole water column circulates. Mount a 150 W heater near the outlet.
  5. Add hardscape and plantsUse the height: tall stems or vallisneria at the back, vertical driftwood for angelfish to weave through, low plants in front. See the plant care guide.
  6. Fill and condition the waterFill over a plate and dose dechlorinator for about 26 gallons. See preparing water for a fish tank.
  7. Cycle the tankEstablish filter bacteria first — usually 3–6 weeks. See how to cycle a fish tank and the cycle tracker.
  8. Confirm water parametersAmmonia and nitrite at zero within 24 hours of dosing, nitrate present. Check pH and hardness against your chosen species — see aquarium water parameters.
  9. Add fish in stagesSchool and bottom group first, centerpiece last. Adding everything at once risks new tank syndrome.
  10. Monitor the first monthsTest weekly for two months and whenever fish behave oddly.

Equipment for a 29 Gallon Aquarium

A 29 gallon takes mid-size equipment. The main difference from a 20 long is that light and flow have to reach 18 inches down.

🌀Filter

Aim for four to six times the volume per hour — about 120–180 GPH of rated flow — from a hang-on-back filter labelled for 30–55 gallons or a small canister. Size toward the top for goldfish, cichlids or livebearer colonies. Place the intake low and the outlet near the surface so the tall water column turns over.

🌡️Heater

A 29 gallon in a 70°F room heated to 78°F needs about 100–150 W; one 150 W heater or two 75 W heaters suit most homes. A goldfish tank needs none. Use a separate thermometer.

💡Lighting

A 30-inch fixture fits both the 29 and the 20 long. For fish only, any decent LED works. For plants, the 18-inch depth needs a stronger light than a 20 long to reach the substrate — low-light plants are easy, carpets are hard. Run 6–8 hours on a timer.

🪨Substrate

About 35–45 lb of gravel or sand for a 2-inch layer across 30 × 12 inches. Sand suits corydoras; a sloped bed makes the narrow tank look deeper.

🪵Stand

A running 29 gallon weighs about 320 lb. Use a purpose-built 30 × 12 inch stand; it also fits a 20 long. Never use household furniture at this weight.

Water Parameters for a 29 Gallon Aquarium

A 29 gallon has no “correct” water chemistry of its own. An angelfish community wants soft-to-moderate water at 78–80°F; a goldfish tank wants 65–72°F; a livebearer tank prefers hard, alkaline water. Pick species whose ranges overlap with your tap water.

The 29's volume makes it noticeably more stable than a 20, which is why it is a common recommendation for a first “serious” tank. Ammonia and nitrite at zero, and nitrate kept low through water changes, are the universal targets.

Test GH, KH and pH from the tap before choosing fish. Our fish tank water guide and what kind of water for a fish tank explain the options; cloudy aquarium water and how to lower nitrates cover common problems.

29 Gallon Fish Tank Maintenance

A 29 gallon is manageable with a bucket and siphon, though a hose-fed water changer saves effort. Use this framework and let nitrate readings adjust it.

  • Partial water changes. 25% weekly (about 6–7 gallons). Goldfish tanks often need 30–40%. The water change schedule tool sets the rhythm from your readings.
  • Substrate cleaning. Vacuum open areas weekly; a long siphon tube helps reach the bottom of an 18-inch tank.
  • Filter maintenance. Rinse sponges in old tank water every 3–4 weeks.
  • Algae. A long-handled scraper reaches the bottom of the glass; keep lights to 6–8 hours.
  • Testing. Ammonia, nitrite and nitrate weekly for three months; the water parameter checker explains results and the aquarium log tracks them.
  • Top-ups. Replace evaporation with dechlorinated water weekly.

Is a 29 Gallon Fish Tank Right for You?

The 29 gallon is a popular step up from a 20: same footprint, more water, and room for the angelfish that so many keepers want.

✅ Advantages

  • Height for angelfish. The smallest common tank that fits an adult pair.
  • Stable volume. About 26 actual gallons buffers mistakes well.
  • Shared footprint. Lids, lights and stands fit a 20 long too.
  • Two fancy goldfish. Fits a pair with strong filtration.
  • Tall planted layouts. Room for stems and vallisneria to grow.

⚠️ Considerations

  • No extra length. Active schools have the same 30-inch lane as a 20 long.
  • Narrow floor. One bottom group is the comfortable limit.
  • Harder to clean. An 18-inch reach to the substrate.
  • Weight. About 320 lb running; needs a proper stand.
  • Lighting plants. Light must reach 18 inches down.

Choose a 29 gallon if you want an angelfish pair, two fancy goldfish or a tall planted tank. Choose a 20 long instead if your fish are small and active — you lose little. Choose a 40 breeder if you want more floor and swimming room for the same kind of community.

29 Gallon Fish Tank FAQ

What are the dimensions of a 29 gallon fish tank?

A standard 29 gallon fish tank is about 30 inches long, 12 inches front to back and 18 inches tall (76 × 30 × 46 cm). It shares its footprint with the 20 gallon long. A 30 gallon is usually 36 × 12 × 16 inches, and a 30 breeder 36 × 18 × 12 inches.

How much does a 29 gallon fish tank weigh?

About 40–50 lb (18–23 kg) empty and about 300–340 lb (136–154 kg) filled and running: roughly 45 lb of glass, 217 lb of water (26 actual gallons), 35–45 lb of gravel and some décor. A typical setup comes to about 320 lb.

How many fish can I put in a 29 gallon tank?

Roughly 15–20 small community fish, for example a pair of angelfish, 10 rummy-nose tetras and 6 sterbai corydoras. That assumes a filter rated for the tank and weekly water changes.

Can angelfish live in a 29 gallon tank?

Yes, one pair. The angelfish tank size calculator treats 29 gallons as the minimum for a pair, because the 18-inch height just meets an adult's requirement. A 40 breeder is recommended for more room.

How many guppies can live in a 29 gallon tank?

About 14–16 adult guppies — enough for a mixed-sex colony with plants, or guppies alongside corydoras.

Can goldfish live in a 29 gallon tank?

One or two fancy goldfish, with strong filtration and no tropical tank mates. Single-tail goldfish need at least 40 gallons and ideally a pond.

What is the difference between a 29 and a 30 gallon tank?

A 29 is 30 × 12 × 18 inches; a 30 gallon is usually 36 × 12 × 16 inches. The 30 is 6 inches longer and 2 inches shorter, which suits active swimmers better. The 29 suits tall fish like angelfish.

What size heater do I need for a 29 gallon tank?

About 100–150 W; one 150 W heater or two 75 W heaters suit most rooms. Goldfish tanks usually need none.

What size filter do I need for a 29 gallon tank?

About 120–180 GPH of rated flow from a hang-on-back filter rated for 30–55 gallons or a small canister, with the intake low and the outlet near the surface.

Is a 29 gallon tank good for beginners?

Yes. It holds more stable conditions than a 20 and fits popular fish like angelfish. The main drawback is the 18-inch reach when cleaning.

How FishCare AI Makes Tank Recommendations

The species, group sizes and stocking combinations on this page are based on the same factors the Aquarium Planner and Fish Compatibility Checker weigh for every species in our database:

  • Adult size — we plan around the fish an animal becomes, not the juvenile sold in stores.
  • Swimming behaviour — cruising, hovering and darting species need different lengths and depths.
  • Territorial requirements — cichlids and some catfish claim floor space that has to exist in the footprint.
  • Schooling needs — shoaling species are counted as groups, never as individuals.
  • Bioload — body mass, diet and messiness drive the filtration and water-change budget.
  • Aquarium footprint — length, width and surface area, not just gallons.
  • Compatibility — temperament, size difference and fin-nipping risk between species.
  • Water requirements — temperature, pH and hardness overlap across every fish in a combination.

For the 29 gallon fish tank specifically, recommendations assume the standard 30 × 12 × 18 inch footprint. Species that need more than 30 inches of swimming length or a wider floor were excluded or flagged, while species that use height were favoured. Per-species numbers match the species tank size calculators.

Dimensions and empty weights are typical figures for the size class, not specifications for any particular product; filled weights are estimates built from the assumptions shown. Husbandry ranges are general guidance drawn from mainstream aquarium practice and our editorial policy, not veterinary or scientific advice. Individual fish, tank layouts and local water vary — check any plan against your own parameters before buying fish.
