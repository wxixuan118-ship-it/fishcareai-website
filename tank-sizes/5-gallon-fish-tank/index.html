5 Gallon Fish Tank: Dimensions, Weight, Stocking & Best Fish

A standard 5 gallon fish tank measures about 16 × 8 × 10 inches (41 × 20 × 25 cm), weighs roughly 7 lb (3 kg) empty and about 60 lb (27 kg) once filled with water, gravel and décor, and is best for a single betta or a colony of dwarf shrimp. It holds about 19 liters.

Five gallons is the smallest tank that can run a heater and a cycled filter properly, which is why the betta tank size calculator treats it as the minimum for one male betta. It is not a small community tank: a school of fish needs swimming room and a bioload buffer that 19 liters cannot provide. Planned around one animal or a shrimp colony, though, a 5 gallon can be a beautiful, low-cost desk aquarium.

5 Gallon Fish Tank Dimensions

“5 gallon” covers more shapes than any other size: rimmed rectangles, cubes, rimless nano tanks and all-in-one kits with the filter hidden in a back chamber. They hold similar water but differ in footprint and in how much of that water the fish can actually use.

Why length matters even in a betta tank

A betta patrols its territory along the length of the tank, so the 16-inch rectangle gives it more usable room than a cube of the same volume. Cubes suit shrimp, which use the floor and plants rather than open water. All-in-one kits are convenient, but subtract the filter chamber: some “5 gallon” kits hold closer to 4 gallons in the display area.

If you are choosing between a 5 and a 10 gallon, the 10 is almost always the better buy: the betta calculator recommends it, it holds temperature and water quality twice as well, and it costs little more.

How Much Does a 5 Gallon Fish Tank Weigh?

Freshwater weighs about 8.34 lb per US gallon (3.78 kg per gallon), so 5 gallons of water would be 42 lb. A running 5 gallon holds a little less, plus the glass, gravel and décor.

Call it about 60 lb — light enough for most desks and dressers, but not for a wall shelf or a wobbly side table. A bare-bottom tank comes in near 50 lb.

Placement safety: place it on solid, level furniture that supports the whole base, away from windows, radiators and air vents. In 19 liters, sunlight or a heating vent can change the temperature by several degrees within hours.

How Many Fish Can Live in a 5 Gallon Tank?

A 5 gallon tank suits one male betta, or a colony of dwarf shrimp, plus a snail or two. That is the honest answer. The betta tank size calculator treats 5 gallons as the minimum for one betta with “no room for tank mates beyond a snail”, and the guppy calculator rates 5 gallons as not recommended for a group.

The “one inch per gallon” rule would allow five 1-inch fish, but it ignores the two things that make small tanks hard: very little water to dilute waste, and no room for a school to swim. In a tank this size, even a few extra fish can push ammonia up within days.

What actually sets the limit in 5 gallons

  • Volume. About 4.5 actual gallons. One missed water change or a dead snail shows up quickly.
  • Swimming room. Sixteen inches is enough for a betta's patrol, not for a school.
  • Temperature. Small volumes cool and heat fast; a heater with a thermostat is essential for tropical fish.
  • Filtration. Gentle flow only — bettas and shrimp struggle against strong currents.

Practical examples for a 5 gallon

  • Works well: 1 male betta and a nerite snail in a planted tank.
  • Works well: 10–15 cherry shrimp to start, growing into a colony, with a nerite snail.
  • Borderline: 6 chili rasboras in a mature, heavily planted tank with no other fish — workable for experienced keepers, but a 10 gallon is far better.
  • Does not work: a betta with tank mates, a goldfish, guppies, or a tetra school.

5 Gallon Fish Tank Stocking Ideas

Four setups that work in a standard 16 × 8 × 10 inch 5 gallon tank. Each is built around one animal or one colony — not a community.

Planted Betta Tank

The classic 5 gallon
Centerpiece1 male betta
Clean-up1 nerite snail
Equipment25 W heater at 78–80°F, sponge filter
HardscapeAnubias, java fern, floating plants, a leaf hammock near the surface

Why it works: Everything a betta needs and nothing it has to compete with. Live plants absorb waste and give the betta places to rest and explore.

Watch for: Keep the flow gentle — a strong filter leaves a long-finned betta struggling. Weekly 30% water changes, per the betta calculator.

Cherry Shrimp Colony

The lowest-effort 5 gallon
Invertebrates10–15 cherry shrimp to start
Clean-up1 nerite snail
EquipmentSponge filter; a heater only if the room drops below about 65°F
HardscapeJava moss, small driftwood, leaf litter

Why it works: Cherry shrimp breed readily at room temperature and produce very little waste. Without fish, every baby survives, and the colony grows into a busy, colourful tank.

Watch for: Copper-based medications and some fertilisers kill shrimp. Add shrimp only to a fully cycled tank.

Desk Aquascape

Plants first, a few shrimp for life
PlantsMosses, small cryptocorynes, a carpet of Monte Carlo or dwarf hairgrass
Invertebrates6–10 cherry shrimp
EquipmentRimless 36 cm tank, nano LED, small hang-on-back with intake sponge

Why it works: A rimless 36 cm tank is the standard aquascaper's nano. Shrimp keep algae down without disturbing the plants. See the plant care guide.

Watch for: Carpeting plants need strong light, which also grows algae — start at 6 hours and adjust.

Chili Rasbora Nano (Experienced)

A tiny school, only in a mature tank
Schooling6 chili rasboras
Equipment25 W heater, sponge filter
HardscapeDense planting, floating plants, dark substrate

Why it works: Chili rasboras are small enough to school in 16 inches and have a tiny bioload. In a heavily planted, long-established tank, this can work for an experienced keeper.

Watch for: This is the edge of what 5 gallons can hold. Do not add a betta or any other fish. A 10 gallon gives the same school far more stability.

Note: these are planning templates, not guarantees. Small tanks change quickly — test water weekly and keep a spare container of dechlorinated water ready for emergency changes.

Fish That Are Not Suitable for a 5 Gallon Tank

Most fish sold as “small” still outgrow a 5 gallon or need a school that it cannot hold. These are the most common mistakes.

✕

Goldfish

Even fancy goldfish reach 6–8 inches and produce heavy waste. A 5 gallon is not a goldfish tank at any stage.

20 gallons for the first fancy goldfish.

✕

Guppies

The guppy calculator rates 5 gallons as not recommended — too small for a stable group, and mixed groups breed quickly.

5–6 males in a 10 gallon.

✕

Neon tetras and other schools

Need a group of six or more and room to swim.

A 10 gallon for nano schools, a 20 for neons.

✕

Betta with tank mates

The betta calculator allows no tank mates beyond a snail at 5 gallons.

A 10 gallon for a betta with a few calm nano fish.

✕

Mollies and platies

Larger livebearers that breed constantly and produce a lot of waste.

20 gallons or more.

✕

Zebra danios

Fast schooling fish that need a long tank.

A 20 gallon long.

✕

Plecos

Even a bristlenose reaches 4–5 inches.

A nerite snail handles algae in a 5 gallon.

A single pea puffer is sometimes suggested for 5 gallons; it is possible in a heavily planted species-only tank, but it eats shrimp and snails and needs frozen food, so it is not a beginner choice.

How to Set Up a 5 Gallon Fish Tank

Setting up a 5 gallon follows the standard freshwater sequence — our how to set up a fish tank guide covers the fundamentals. The steps below add what matters most for a very small tank.

  1. Choose the locationSolid, level furniture away from windows and heat sources, near an outlet.
  2. Rinse and place the tankRinse with plain water only. Check that the lid fits — bettas and shrimp can jump or climb out.
  3. Add substrateAbout 10–12 lb of rinsed gravel or sand for 1.5 inches, or a plant substrate for a planted tank.
  4. Install filter and heaterA sponge filter with a small air pump is ideal: gentle flow and shrimp-safe. Add a 25 W adjustable heater for a betta.
  5. Add plants and décorLive plants make a small tank far more stable. Anubias, java fern and mosses suit low light. See the plant care guide.
  6. Fill and condition the waterDose dechlorinator for about 4.5 gallons. See preparing water for a fish tank.
  7. Cycle the tankEven a 5 gallon must be cycled before adding a betta — usually 3–6 weeks. See how to cycle a fish tank and the cycle tracker.
  8. Confirm water parametersAmmonia and nitrite at zero within 24 hours of dosing, nitrate present. See aquarium water parameters.
  9. Add the betta or shrimpAcclimate slowly — small tanks have little margin for temperature or pH shock.
  10. Monitor closelyTest twice a week for the first month. In 5 gallons, problems appear within days.

Equipment for a 5 Gallon Aquarium

A 5 gallon needs very little equipment, but each piece matters more than in a large tank.

🌀Filter

About 20–30 GPH of gentle flow. A sponge filter run by a small air pump is the best choice for bettas and shrimp: it is gentle, shrimp-safe and grows plenty of bacteria. Small hang-on-back filters work if you add a sponge to the intake and baffle the outlet.

🌡️Heater

A 25 W adjustable heater is enough for a betta in most rooms. Avoid larger heaters — in 4.5 gallons a heater that sticks on can overheat the water quickly — and avoid fixed “preset” heaters. Shrimp tanks at room temperature may need none. Use a separate thermometer.

Water Parameters for a 5 Gallon Aquarium

A betta wants 78–80°F and soft-to-moderate water; cherry shrimp are happy at room temperature in most tap water. Pick the setup that matches your tap water and room temperature.

The challenge of a 5 gallon is speed: with about 4.5 gallons of water, temperature, ammonia and pH all change much faster than in larger tanks. That is the main reason the betta calculator recommends 10 gallons. Ammonia and nitrite at zero, and nitrate kept low, are the universal targets.

5 Gallon Fish Tank Maintenance

A 5 gallon is quick to maintain, but it needs it every week.

  • Partial water changes. About 30% weekly (1–1.5 gallons), as the betta calculator suggests. Match the temperature of the new water closely.
  • Substrate cleaning. A small siphon or turkey baster removes waste from the gravel.
  • Filter maintenance. Squeeze the sponge in old tank water every 2–3 weeks.
  • Top-ups. Evaporation concentrates minerals quickly; top up with dechlorinated water every few days.
  • Testing. Weekly ammonia, nitrite and nitrate; the water parameter checker explains results.

Feed a betta a few pellets once or twice a day; remove anything uneaten. The fish feeding calculator helps size portions.

Is a 5 Gallon Fish Tank Right for You?

The 5 gallon is a specialist tank: excellent for one betta or a shrimp colony, poor for anything else.

✅ Advantages

  • Fits anywhere. About 60 lb running on a 16 × 8 inch footprint.
  • Cheap to set up. A 25 W heater and a sponge filter.
  • Ideal for shrimp. A colony thrives with little effort.
  • Meets the betta minimum. The calculator's minimum for one male.

⚠️ Considerations

  • Very little margin for error. Temperature and water quality change fast.
  • No community. One betta or shrimp — not a school.
  • Weekly maintenance is not optional.
  • Kit sizes vary. Some “5 gallon” kits hold closer to 4 gallons.

Choose a 5 gallon if you want one betta or a shrimp colony and have limited space. Choose a 10 gallon instead if you can fit it — it is the betta calculator's recommended size and holds a small school too. Choose a 20 gallon if you want a first community.

5 Gallon Fish Tank FAQ

What are the dimensions of a 5 gallon fish tank?

A standard 5 gallon fish tank is about 16 inches long, 8 inches front to back and 10 inches tall (41 × 20 × 25 cm). Cubes are about 11 inches on each side, and rimless 36 cm tanks measure 36 × 22 × 26 cm. Kits vary, so check the actual measurements.

How much does a 5 gallon fish tank weigh?

About 6–8 lb (3–4 kg) empty and about 55–70 lb (25–32 kg) filled and running: roughly 7 lb of glass, 38 lb of water (4.5 actual gallons), 10–12 lb of gravel and a little décor. A typical setup comes to about 60 lb.

How many fish can I put in a 5 gallon tank?

One male betta, or a colony of dwarf shrimp, plus a snail. The betta tank size calculator treats 5 gallons as the minimum for one betta with no tank mates beyond a snail. Schools of fish need at least 10 gallons.

Is a 5 gallon tank big enough for a betta?

Yes, as a minimum — with a heater, a gentle filter and weekly 30% water changes. A 10 gallon is the recommended size because it holds temperature and water quality much better.

Can a betta have tank mates in a 5 gallon?

Only a snail, such as a nerite. Fish tank mates need at least a 10 gallon.

Can goldfish live in a 5 gallon tank?

No. Even fancy goldfish grow to 6–8 inches and need about 20 gallons for the first fish.

What size heater do I need for a 5 gallon tank?

An adjustable 25 W heater. Avoid larger heaters, which can overheat a small tank quickly if they stick on.

What filter is best for a 5 gallon tank?

A sponge filter with a small air pump — gentle, shrimp-safe and good for bacteria. A small hang-on-back works with an intake sponge and a baffled outlet.

How often should I change the water in a 5 gallon tank?

About 30% (1–1.5 gallons) weekly, matching the temperature of the new water. Test weekly; small tanks change quickly.

How FishCare AI Makes Tank Recommendations

The species, group sizes and stocking combinations on this page are based on the same factors the Aquarium Planner and Fish Compatibility Checker weigh for every species in our database:

  • Adult size — we plan around the fish an animal becomes, not the juvenile sold in stores.
  • Swimming behaviour — cruising, hovering and darting species need different lengths and depths.
  • Territorial requirements — cichlids and some catfish claim floor space that has to exist in the footprint.
  • Schooling needs — shoaling species are counted as groups, never as individuals.
  • Bioload — body mass, diet and messiness drive the filtration and water-change budget.
  • Aquarium footprint — length, width and surface area, not just gallons.
  • Compatibility — temperament, size difference and fin-nipping risk between species.
  • Water requirements — temperature, pH and hardness overlap across every fish in a combination.

For the 5 gallon fish tank specifically, recommendations assume the standard 16 × 8 × 10 inch tank. Only animals that can live their full lives in about 4.5 gallons of actual water were recommended; per-species numbers match the species tank size calculators.

Dimensions and empty weights are typical figures for the size class, not specifications for any particular product; filled weights are estimates built from the assumptions shown. Husbandry ranges are general guidance drawn from mainstream aquarium practice and our editorial policy, not veterinary or scientific advice. Individual fish, tank layouts and local water vary — check any plan against your own parameters before buying fish.
