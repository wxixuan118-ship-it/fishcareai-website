What goldfish tank size do you need?

Goldfish tank size depends on one question first: fancy or single-tail? Goldfish are among the most misunderstood fish in the hobby. Sold for cents in pet stores, they are routinely placed in bowls and expected to thrive. A goldfish in a bowl typically lives 3–6 months. The same fish in a proper filtered, cycled tank can live 15–25 years and grow to 12 inches or more.

Single-tail goldfish (common, comet, shubunkin) are active, fast-growing fish that reach 10–14 inches as adults. They need a minimum of 40 gallons for the first fish and 10 gallons for each additional fish. Ponds are ideal for adult single-tail goldfish.

Fancy goldfish (oranda, ryukin, ranchu, black moor, telescope eye) are slower swimmers and stay smaller — typically 6–8 inches. They need a minimum of 20 gallons for the first fish and 10 gallons per additional fish. Never mix fancy and single-tail goldfish in the same tank: their swimming speeds and feeding behaviours are incompatible.

This goldfish tank size calculator uses those two starting points — 20 gallons for a fancy, 40 for a single-tail — and adds 10 gallons for every goldfish after the first. Goldfish produce exceptional amounts of ammonia and waste. All goldfish tanks need heavy filtration — a filter rated for twice the tank size is a common starting point. They also prefer cool water (65–72°F / 18–22°C) and should never be kept with tropical fish.

How many goldfish in a 20 gallon tank, 40 gallon tank or larger?

The table applies the same rule the calculator uses — 20 gallons for the first fancy or 40 for the first single-tail, then 10 gallons per additional goldfish — assuming a cycled tank with a filter rated for at least twice its volume and weekly water changes. Sharing the tank with other fish? Goldfish want 65–72°F water, which rules out tropical community fish, and they will eat anything small. Other goldfish of the same type, white cloud minnows, dojo loaches and large snails are the realistic options. Check any pairing in the goldfish tank mates hub or the fish compatibility checker.

Why the numbers are so large for a "beginner" fish: Carassius auratus (Wikipedia) is a carp. It grows for most of its 10–25 year life, has no stomach and eats constantly, and produces more ammonia per inch than almost any other aquarium fish. Volume and filtration are the only things that keep that in check.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum tank size for a goldfish?
The minimum goldfish tank size depends on the type. Fancy goldfish (oranda, ryukin, ranchu) need at least 20 gallons for one fish, with 10 gallons per additional fish. Single-tail goldfish (common, comet, shubunkin) need at least 40 gallons for one fish, plus 10 gallons each. These minimums assume a cycled tank with a powerful filter.
Can goldfish live in a bowl?
No. Bowls have no room for a filter or heater, cannot be cycled, and the tiny water volume means ammonia spikes within hours of feeding. Goldfish in bowls also outgrow their housing rapidly. A goldfish in a bowl lives weeks to months. The same fish in a proper tank lives 15–25 years. Bowls are not an acceptable long-term home for any fish.
How many gallons does a comet or common goldfish need?
A single comet or common goldfish needs a minimum of 40 US gallons. These fish reach 10–14 inches as adults and are built for open water — ponds are the ideal long-term habitat. For each additional single-tail goldfish, add at least 10 gallons, though ponds or very large tanks are far more appropriate for groups.
How many fancy goldfish can I keep in a 20-gallon tank?
One fancy goldfish in a 20-gallon tank. Goldfish are heavy waste producers, and the 20-gallon volume is already the bare minimum for a single fish with a powerful filter. Adding a second fish to a 20-gallon tank will cause chronic water quality problems even with frequent water changes. A 30-gallon tank is a more comfortable home for one fancy goldfish.
How many goldfish fit in a 55-gallon tank?
A 55-gallon tank can hold 3–4 fancy goldfish comfortably (20 gallons for the first, 10 gallons each thereafter: 20+10+10+10 = 50 gallons for 4). It is too small for adult single-tail goldfish, which need ponds. Always oversize the filter — for goldfish, run a filter rated for at least twice the tank volume.
