What tank size does a Angelfish need?

Freshwater angelfish are tall, laterally compressed fish that grow to 6 inches in body length and up to 10 inches tall including their extended fins. They need a tank with adequate vertical space — a minimum of 18 inches of water height, with 24 inches preferred.

A single pair of angelfish needs a minimum of 29 gallons — a standard 29-gallon tank is exactly 18 inches tall, meeting the minimum height requirement. A 40-gallon breeder is the preferred starter tank for a pair because it provides more floor space for territories.

For a group of angelfish (5–6 fish), a 55- to 75-gallon tall tank is appropriate. Angelfish establish a social hierarchy in groups and are less aggressive when they have room to form natural pairs and defend small territories. Overstocking is a common cause of fin damage and aggression in angelfish tanks.

Angelfish become significantly more aggressive when breeding. A breeding pair will defend a large territory and may kill or injure tank mates during spawning. A dedicated 40-gallon tank per breeding pair avoids this problem.

Frequently asked questions

What size tank do angelfish need?
A single pair of angelfish needs a minimum of 29 gallons, which provides the 18 inches of water height angelfish require. A 40-gallon breeder tank is the widely recommended starting point for a pair. For a group of 5–6 angelfish, a 55- to 75-gallon tall tank is appropriate.
Can angelfish live in a 20-gallon tank?
A standard 20-gallon tall tank is not suitable for adult angelfish long-term. The tank is too narrow and too short in height for a species that reaches 10 inches tall. A 20-gallon high tank is sometimes used as a temporary grow-out for juveniles, but adult angelfish need at least a 29-gallon tank.
How many angelfish can I keep in a 55-gallon tank?
A 55-gallon tank can comfortably house 4–6 angelfish, depending on the tank dimensions. Tall 55-gallon tanks (48 x 13 x 20 inches) work well for angelfish groups. The social hierarchy in a group of 5–6 is usually stable once pairs form, but the tank should have plants or décor to break lines of sight.
Do angelfish need a tall or long tank?
Angelfish benefit most from height. They are naturally tall fish that swim through vertical space and often rest near the surface or mid-water column. The minimum water height is 18 inches; 24 inches is preferred. A 29-gallon tall, 40-gallon breeder, or 55-gallon tall tank all work well.
Can I keep just one angelfish?
A single angelfish can live alone if the tank is large enough (40+ gallons) with peaceful, compatible tank mates. Angelfish kept alone sometimes do well in community tanks. However, a solitary angelfish is more likely to become aggressive toward tank mates than one kept in a small group where it can establish natural social bonds.
