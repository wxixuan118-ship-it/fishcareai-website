What betta fish tank size do you need?

Betta fish tank size has one firm floor and one strong recommendation. A single male betta needs a minimum of 5 US gallons, but 10 gallons is the most widely recommended starting size. The extra volume stabilises temperature, dilutes waste between water changes, and gives the fish more territory to patrol — all of which reduce chronic stress and disease risk.

This betta fish tank size calculator treats one male as the default and switches to sorority sizing — 20 gallons for 5 females, plus 5 gallons per extra female — once you enter five or more fish. Female bettas can be kept together in a sorority, but only with 5 or more females in a 20-gallon tank with dense planting and multiple hiding spots. Groups of 2–4 females create uneven aggression hierarchies and should be avoided. A 29- or 40-gallon planted tank with 6–8 females is the most stable sorority setup.

Never keep two males together in any size tank. Even a divider is only a short-term solution; the constant visual stress from a male on the other side causes chronic fin damage and shortened lifespan.

How many betta fish in a 5 gallon tank, 10 gallon tank or larger?

For a male the answer is always one, whatever the size; the table shows what else each tank size makes possible. Sorority counts assume a cycled, heavily planted tank with line-of-sight breaks and at least five females. Sharing the tank with other fish? A male betta can share a 10-gallon or larger tank with calm, short-finned species that will not nip — corydoras, kuhli loaches, snails, small rasboras. Avoid guppies (the tails trigger attacks) and anything larger and faster that will outcompete the betta at feeding. Check any pairing in the betta tank mates hub or the fish compatibility checker.

Why 5 gallons and not a bowl: Betta splendens (Wikipedia) breathes air from the surface, which is why bowls seem to work — but the fish still needs 76–82°F water and zero ammonia, and only a filtered, heated tank of 5 gallons or more can hold both steady between water changes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum tank size for a betta fish?
The minimum betta fish tank size is 5 US gallons for a single male. Tanks smaller than 5 gallons cannot hold a proper heater or filter, which causes dangerous temperature swings and ammonia spikes. A betta in a 1-gallon bowl often lives 3–6 months; the same fish in a cycled, heated 10-gallon tank typically lives 3–5 years.
Can a betta fish live in a 1-gallon or 2-gallon tank?
No. Bowls and nano tanks under 5 gallons cannot support stable temperature or adequate filtration. Ammonia rises sharply within days in an uncycled small container. The stress leads to fin rot, ich, and early death. The 5-gallon minimum is the scientific and hobbyist consensus minimum, not a preference.
How many betta fish can I keep in a 10-gallon tank?
One male betta per tank, always. Male bettas are highly territorial and will fight to the death if housed together. A 10-gallon tank can house a single male comfortably, or serve as a starter sorority for 4–5 female bettas with dense planting and caves.
What size tank is needed for a female betta sorority?
A female betta sorority needs at least 20 gallons with a minimum of 5 females, heavy planting with line-of-sight breaks, and multiple hiding spots. Fewer than 5 females concentrate aggression on one or two fish. A 29- to 40-gallon tank with 6–8 females is more stable and easier to manage.
Is a 5-gallon tank good enough for a betta?
A 5-gallon tank meets the minimum requirement but leaves little margin for error. Water parameters shift quickly in small volumes, and a betta has limited swimming space. A 10-gallon tank costs little more, holds temperature and chemistry more stably, and gives the fish a noticeably better quality of life. If you can choose, choose 10 gallons.
