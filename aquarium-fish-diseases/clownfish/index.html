Quick answer

What are the most common clownfish health problems?

The most common clownfish health problems are parasites and stress. The main diseases are marine ich (Cryptocaryon), marine velvet (Amyloodinium), Brooklynella — often called "clownfish disease" — Uronema, and gill or skin flukes, plus secondary bacterial infections.

The one rule that matters most: fast or heavy breathing combined with dust, slime or peeling skin is an emergency. Velvet and Brooklynella can kill a clownfish within days, so move the fish to a quarantine tank and start treatment the same day — don't wait to "see how it goes."

Most other signs — pale color, not eating, hiding — come from stress or water quality. Test ammonia, nitrite, salinity and temperature first; it takes five minutes and rules out the most common cause.

Bacterial infections (fin rot, red streaks, popeye) are usually secondary — they follow parasites, injury or poor water. Fix the trigger first; use antibiotics only in a hospital tank, since they damage the biofilter.

Step 4 · Treat

How to treat a sick clownfish safely

  1. Test the water first. Ammonia and nitrite must read 0; check salinity and temperature. Fix anything off before adding medication.
  2. Move the fish to a quarantine tank. A bare 10–20 gallon tank with a heater, air stone and a PVC pipe to hide in. Most medications harm corals, invertebrates and the display's biofilter.
  3. Confirm the diagnosis. Use the table above. If you can't tell ich from velvet, treat it as velvet — it's the faster killer.
  4. Treat at the full, measured dose. Copper only works while free copper stays at 0.15–0.20 mg/L[3], so measure it with a test kit at least daily.
  5. Leave the display tank fishless. Parasites need a host to survive. Skip this step and the disease returns as soon as the fish goes back.

Don't: dose copper in a reef tank, mix medications, or use freshwater-aquarium remedies such as aquarium salt or peas — clownfish are marine fish.

Prevention

Water parameters that keep clownfish healthy

Temp74–80°F
Salinity1.020–1.025
pH8.0–8.4
Ammonia / NO₂0 ppm
Nitrate< 20 ppm

Most outbreaks start with a new fish. Quarantine every new arrival for at least 4 weeks, buy captive-bred clownfish where possible, and keep salinity and temperature steady — sudden swings weaken the slime coat that blocks parasites.

Clownfish disease FAQ

What are clownfish health problems usually caused by?

Most start with stress — a new tank, unstable salinity or temperature, or an aggressive tank mate — which weakens the slime coat and lets parasites such as ich, velvet and Brooklynella take hold. Unquarantined new fish are the most common source.

What is "clownfish disease"?

It's the common name for Brooklynella hostilis, a ciliate parasite that causes thick white slime and peeling skin. It's especially common in wild-caught clownfish and can kill within days, so formalin treatment should start the day you notice it[4].

How do I tell marine ich from velvet?

Ich forms separate white dots about the size of salt grains. Velvet looks like a fine gold or grey powder that's easiest to see with a flashlight from the side, and it comes with much faster breathing. If in doubt, treat as velvet.

Why is my clownfish not eating?

In a new fish it's usually stress and settles in 3–5 days. In an established fish, test water and look for spots, slime or rapid breathing — loss of appetite is often the first sign of a parasite.

Can I treat clownfish diseases in a reef tank?

No. Copper and formalin are toxic to corals and invertebrates, and parasites will survive in the display anyway. Treat in a separate quarantine tank and leave the display fishless.

How long does a clownfish take to recover?

It depends on the disease. Copper treatment for marine ich runs for at least 3–6 weeks[1], and the fish should stay in quarantine for at least two weeks after the last symptom before going back.

Is my clownfish sick or just wiggling?

Clownfish naturally shimmy and "wiggle" near their host anemone or a favorite spot. That's normal if the fish eats well, has clear skin and breathes calmly.
