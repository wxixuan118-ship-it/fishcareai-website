Freshwater fish care

Freshwater fish care begins with a properly sized and cycled aquarium. Before adding fish, make sure the tank has suitable filtration and an established nitrogen cycle, and test ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, temperature and pH regularly.

Requirements vary sharply between species. Betta fish generally prefer warm water and gentle flow. Goldfish produce large amounts of waste and need strong filtration. Discus require warm, stable water, while schooling fish such as many tetras should be kept in appropriate groups. FishCare AI publishes species-specific fish care guides so you can research those differences rather than applying one rule to every aquarium fish.

Fish health resources

Is a fish not eating, hiding, gasping at the surface, swimming sideways or showing white spots? Behaviour gives useful clues, but similar symptoms have many different causes. The fish health library covers common problems, symptoms, likely causes and prevention.

Warning signs worth acting on include loss of appetite, rapid breathing, gasping at the surface, white spots, clamped or torn fins, unusual hiding, faded colour, bloating, buoyancy problems, rubbing against objects and erratic swimming.

Before assuming a specific disease, check the environment. Poor water quality, ammonia or nitrite exposure, wrong temperature, low oxygen, aggression, overcrowding and sudden chemistry changes all cause stress. These fish health resources are educational and are not a substitute for diagnosis or treatment by a qualified aquatic veterinarian.

Fish care for beginners

New to keeping fish? Fish care for beginners comes down to a few essentials.

First, choose an aquarium large enough for the adult size and number of fish you plan to keep. Second, establish the nitrogen cycle before fully stocking it: beneficial bacteria convert toxic ammonia into nitrite and then nitrate, and in a cycled aquarium ammonia and nitrite should both read 0 ppm. Third, research every species before buying it, checking adult size, temperature range, pH, diet, temperament, minimum group size and compatibility. Finally, keep a consistent routine of feeding, observation, water testing, partial water changes and equipment maintenance. Those habits are the foundation of a stable, long-term fish tank.

FAQ

Fish care FAQ

Quick answers to common aquarium care questions about feeding, water changes, oxygen, bowls, and fish health.

Most aquarium fish should be fed once or twice daily, only as much as they can eat in about two minutes. Uneaten food should be removed because it breaks down into ammonia and can quickly harm water quality.
Many beginner tanks do well with a 20-30% water change every week or every other week, but the right schedule depends on tank size, fish load, plants, filtration, and nitrate readings. Always use dechlorinated, temperature-matched water.
Ammonia and nitrite should always be 0 ppm. Nitrate should usually stay below 20-40 ppm, depending on the species. Temperature, pH, and hardness should match the fish you keep, and sudden changes should be avoided.
Fish gasping at the surface may indicate low oxygen, ammonia or nitrite poisoning, high temperature, overcrowding, or poor circulation. Increase surface movement, test the water immediately, and stop feeding until the cause is clear.
Most fish should not live in bowls long term because bowls are small, unstable, and usually lack filtration and oxygen exchange. If a fish is already in a bowl, keep water clean with frequent partial changes and plan an upgrade to a filtered aquarium.
Warning signs include clamped fins, white spots, torn fins, rapid breathing, loss of appetite, hiding, floating problems, faded color, or unusual swimming. Test water first because many fish health problems begin with poor water quality.
